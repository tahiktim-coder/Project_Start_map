How it is drawn

Head, torso and collar: keyed maps drawn pixel by pixel from the portraits (a downscaled portrait was used as an underlay, then redrawn). Limbs: drafted from joint angles into keyed maps; in a real pass each would be cleaned by hand. A frame is assembled from parts: far arm, far leg, near leg, body, head, collar front, near arm. Light comes from above and in front of the face; no outlines.

Cost of the full cast

Each person needs about 6 head maps (front, blink, working, bowed, back, lying), 4–5 body maps (standing, from behind, kneeling, seated, lying) and a set of arm and leg poses: about 40 for the nine actions (walk 16, climb 8, kneel 4, carry 6, sit and sleep the rest). Limb sets can be shared by people of the same build, so two sets (slight, heavy) cover the five. That is roughly 55 head and body maps plus 80 limb maps, about 135 hand-made maps, against 14 + 40 on this page. For a human pixel artist that is two to four weeks; for me, five to eight more sessions like this one, most of it spent on the faces, which took six to eight passes each here. Every new costume, injury or state (wet, wounded, in a helmet) is more maps: nothing is generated.

Weaknesses

  • The face carries the likeness only through hair, brows, eye colour and collar. Eyes are one pixel: Cora can look down or blink; nobody can frown, cry or smile.
  • The limbs on this page are drafts made from joint angles and not yet cleaned by hand: knees and toe-off feet have stray pixels at 4× and above.
  • Light is baked into the maps from the front. Walking left mirrors the sprite, so the light flips side.
  • The body is three-quarter but the legs are side-on. It holds at 3×; at 10× it shows.
  • Cora's hands-behind-the-back stance reads as a hand on the hip at a glance. The console pose barely leans; heads don't tilt.
  • The lamp's warmth on people is a colour shift of the whole figure, not drawn light.
