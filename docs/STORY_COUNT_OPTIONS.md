# The count: what went wrong, and three ways to fix it (2026-09-25)

Nothing here is built. This is for the designer to choose from.

## My recommendation: ONE MORE, with the critic's fixes

A.U.R.A. is right. Four crew boarded hull 9. **You are the one she brought aboard on the way.**

- **The truth.** Hull 9's commander never reached the launch pad, and the window did not wait. A.U.R.A. cannot wake a crew
  without a commanding officer (the wake screen says so at minute one). So before waking anyone she stopped at the first
  wreck on the heading. Its four crew had died in their sleep. The commander's pod runs on its own power, and that
  commander was alive. She cut the pod out, put it in the empty seat, and woke all five. That commander is you.
- **Why nobody knew.** Commanders are put under on Earth and loaded asleep. Crews never meet their commander before the
  wake, and you never saw your ship. Planted in the opening as a plain fact, not a clue. (No amnesia.)
- **The ladder.** Sector 1: the first wreck has four dead in their pods and a frame where a fifth pod was cut out; the disc
  drawing is in its captain's cabin; then the count scene, with no explanation. Sector 2: the sleep log shows one unplanned
  stop, "11 hours at a wreck. Salvage: one sleep pod and its power unit." The crew fear she let that crew die to take their
  commander. Sector 3: Mira dates the dead with the disc's star map. They died years before the stop, so A.U.R.A. is
  cleared, and your pod was sealed years before the other four. Sectors 4-5: the launch ledger. The first wreck's line
  holds the four names Aris read aloud in minute five, and the commander's name is yours. The player makes that match.
- **What it changes on a replay.** The tutorial wreck was your ship. Aris read your first crew's names. The note in the
  disc margin was written to you. "Good morning, Commander" was her first sentence to a stranger she chose.
- **The disc.** It was your first ship's orders, found in your own cabin. In sector 3 it is the clock that dates the dead
  and uncovers the time throw. At the light, cutting it finishes the job your first crew died on the way to do.
- **What the player does.** At the end there are two blanks aboard: your first ship's crew plate, in your cargo since
  sector 1, and hull 9's crew list. You choose where your name goes. Every ending closes on her last count line.

Everything below is the raw material: a first-time player's read of the early game, the three designs, and the critic.

## 1. A first-time player reads the opening and sectors 1-3

**First-time player read of Silent Exodus: opening and sectors 1–3**

## 1. Moments I was confused

**Opening**
- *"the ship has woken all five of you"* followed by *"All four crew are awake and well."* I read this as four crew plus the commander, which is how real ships talk. It didn't strike me as odd at all, so the hook didn't land.
- *"Eight ships, and not one of them ever sent a message home?"* I couldn't tell if Vance was asking or telling me. If everyone knew the eight went silent, why was a ninth ship sent? Nobody reacts.

**Sector 1**
- *"Why is it the only page marked with our heading? — K."* I don't know what "a page marked with a heading" means. I also don't know who K. is. A.U.R.A. later calls Vance "Kael Vance", and the crew plate has "K. ADEYEMI · SECURITY". Is K. Vance? Is K. the security officer on every ship? Nothing tells me.
- *"Gold. Thrown out … in 1977, bolted to a probe the size of a car."* plus *"One of them is waving."* That's the Voyager record mixed up with the Pioneer plaque. Anyone who knows space history will trip on it.
- *"She named you, but she did not count you. You are not on her list."* She just said "And you, Commander." So I am on her list. This reads as a fuss over wording.
- *"Sealed before launch. Before they had picked a commander."* How can a ship launch before its commander is picked? Was I added late? The scene treats this as the answer instead of as a stranger question.
- *"She was eight when we left. She'd be forty-three now."* 8 + 61 years is 69, not 43. In sector 3 Jaxon says *"I'm forty-two"*, so his daughter would be older than him.
- *"I watched more than that being built in one year."* The film said thousands of ships were being sent in every direction. So of course he saw more than nine built. His suspicion doesn't follow, and the game's own option ("they were for other headings") sounds more reasonable than he does.
- *"she reads out eleven names"*: the crew plate shows ships carry four people. Eleven from one wreck?
- *"Five beacons ahead … all earlier Exodus ships."* Sector 2 then says only *"the last of the eight"* is left. Where did the other four come from? This might be a clue, but nobody reacts, so it reads as an error.

**Sector 2**
- *"none of them left us so much as a note."* I found a note in the margin of the disc drawing and a crew plate in sector 1.
- *"Old recordings degrade, Specialist."* Damage doesn't add hundreds of ships to a film. This works if A.U.R.A. is meant to look like she's lying, but nobody calls her on it.
- *"It was reading out the gold disc … The two figures. The map."* You can't read out a picture. Also, which "first ship"? The first of all the thousands, or the first on our heading? It matters a lot.
- Which way did the hundreds on the tape go: ahead of us, or after us? Unclear.

**Sector 3**
- *"The third burn did not finish."* I count two sector jumps. Third of what?
- *"SECTOR 3 Generated. — no name. no stars."* Reads like debug text, or a hint that this is a simulation. I couldn't tell which.
- *"NOBODY ABOARD IS LOOKING AT THIS"*: creepy and good. But who is looking, then? Me? It only linked to the missing fifth crew member after the fact.
- *"The burn finishes. You were in the warp the whole time."* So was the line of ships real, or a vision?
- The ship counts keep changing: *"hundreds"* on the tape, *"Thousands of them"* in the throw, *"Nine hundred"* from Mira, *"Three hundred and eleven"* from Vance. I no longer know how many ships there are.
- *"We were not sent further than the others. We were sent less far back in time."* Nothing in sectors 1–2 set up time travel. I had to reread this line three times. It answers a "how" I hadn't asked yet and opens a "why" nobody touches: why throw ships back in time?
- *"I have no explanation."* (A.U.R.A., sorting the dead.) The captain's log says his ship's computer did explain it. So is A.U.R.A. lying? Nobody notices.
- *"If reaching the end of this heading costs crew, do I continue?"* Our orders were to find a planet. When did our heading get an "end"? The goal of settling a planet has quietly disappeared.

## 3. The question I'm holding at the end of each sector
- **Sector 1:** Several, none sharp.

  - Did they lie about there being only nine ships?

- **Sector 2:** This is the clearest sector. Something out there has the disc and spoke through ship one's computer, and Earth's answer was *"Keep building ships."* Why? Are we being sent toward whatever has it? That's a real question I want answered. The count is forgotten.

  The time-travel answer arrived before I'd formed the question. The sector 2 question (disc, "keep building ships") isn't advanced at all. I'm holding "what's the light" loosely.

## 4. The four-vs-five count
- **When I noticed it:** not at the wake-up; "four crew" plus a commander reads as normal. I noticed only when Vance raised it back on the bridge.
- **How much it intrigued me:** it peaked at *"And you, Commander. Four crew."* That, and A.U.R.A.'s *"Thank you, Commander"* if you let it go, are the best lines in sector 1.
- **How long it lasted:** about three lines. Every follow-up choice gives a boring reason: the list was sealed before a commander was picked. Aris states that as settled. The crew plate brings it back only as "every ship was like this", which feels like standard paperwork.
- **After that:** it's never mentioned again in sectors 2 or 3. The only echo is the "NOBODY ABOARD IS LOOKING AT THIS" card, which I linked to the missing fifth person only after the fact. The story moved on to the disc and memo, then to time travel.
- **The deeper problem:** I sided with Jaxon ("It is a glitch") from the start, so it never felt eerie.

## 5. The gold disc
- **After sector 1:** a history card about Voyager that A.U.R.A. tells me to skip. The margin note is the only hook. I care a little.
- **After sector 2:** now I care. The memo says something has the disc and is talking through ship one's computer. I assume our heading points at it and that the disc is how it found Earth ("Anyone who can count can find us").
- **After sector 3:** it's gone. It's not in the throw, the log, the crew moments or the question A.U.R.A. asks. It sits in cargo and does nothing. The light at the end might be the thing that has it, but nothing says so. It feels like a prop used once to set up the memo, then dropped.

## 6. Verdict
- **Too many mysteries, each dropped when the next arrives.** The count (sector 1), the disc and Earth's twin computers (sector 2), and time travel (sector 3) never connect on screen. Pick one spine question and make each sector's reveal deepen it. The sector 2 question (something has the disc, Earth kept building ships) is the strongest; the count and time travel should feed into it, not replace it.
- **Show before telling.** Let me see the count myself first: a crew screen showing four lights and five occupied sleep pods, with A.U.R.A. saying it twice in normal log lines. Then Vance's "She keeps saying four" becomes something we both noticed, instead of something he noticed offscreen.
- **Don't close a mystery in the scene that opens it.** Cut or delay "sealed before they picked a commander" and Aris's tidy explanation. Leave the blank line unexplained, and bring it back in sectors 2–3 (the twin computer, "nobody aboard is looking at this") so it grows.
- **Stop the arrival card and objective line from spoiling the beat.** "Their numbers are higher and they are a hundred years old" and "one of their captains wrote down why" hand me the answer before I find it. Time travel also needs a seed in sectors 1–2, such as the wreck ages not adding up, or it lands as nonsense. Jaxon shouldn't accept it in one line.
- **Fix the facts that make readers doubt the writer:**
  - Jaxon's daughter's age (8 + 61 is not 43, and he's 42).
  - Ship counts: hundreds, thousands, 900 and 311.
  - "Third burn".
  - "Five beacons ahead" against "the last of the eight".
  - "None of them left a note" after a note was found.
  - The Voyager/Pioneer mix-up.
  - "Generated" on the sector 3 card.
  - Who K. is.

## 2. Three resolutions (designer)

**The short answer:** yes, a new player would be confused. The count scene gives the answer about a minute after asking the question. Mira explains it and A.U.R.A. explains it. The answer is also just paperwork, which is why it feels like it drifted from your idea. Your idea was "there is one more of us than she says." The build turned that into "you weren't written down." Below: how writers usually handle a story like this, what to fix whichever answer you pick, three answers, and whether to show what the fifth is. No files were edited.

## How writers usually carry a story like this

1. **Show normal first, then one wrong thing, repeated before anyone mentions it.** The player should hear "four" two or three times with no comment before a character reacts. *2001: A Space Odyssey* establishes that HAL has never made a mistake before his first wrong prediction, so that one error is frightening. Right now Vance says "she keeps saying four" when the player has heard it once.
2. **Let the player notice first. Characters confirm; they never explain.** No character should mention something the player hasn't seen. The "been counting" feel on the first day breaks this. *Return of the Obra Dinn* and *Her Story* never say "this is odd". The player spots it.
3. **Once the guess is obvious, use suspense instead of surprise.** This is Hitchcock's "bomb under the table" (from his Truffaut interviews): if the audience knows about the bomb, they worry the whole time; if they don't, they only get a few seconds of shock. "Is it me?" arrives within 90 seconds. Don't confirm it and don't fight it. Let the player carry the worry, and hold back the *why* and the *what now*.
4. **Each step answers a small question and sharpens the big one.** Is she broken? No. Then which one of us? Then why? Then what do I do? *Outer Wilds* works this way: each place answers "what happened here?" and leaves you a sharper "why?". *Lost* is the warning: questions piled up and none closed.
5. **Plant clues fairly.** The reveal should add one missing piece and make everything earlier read differently. *The Sixth Sense* (the technique only): on a rewatch you see that only the boy ever talks to Malcolm. Your disc note already has a line like this: "Anyone who can count can find us."
6. **An AI's error feels eerie, not buggy, when it is exact, calm, the same every time, can't be fixed, and has a reason behind it.** If she is sloppy anywhere else, players call it a bug and stop treating her as evidence. GERTY in *Moon*: warm, always correct, and his kindness is what keeps the truth hidden.
7. **The mystery should answer the hero's own story.** In *Firewatch* the conspiracy turns out to be a small human tragedy that mirrors Henry running from his wife's illness. "Why four" has to be about the commander, not about Earth's filing.
8. **The player acts out the payoff.** In *Obra Dinn* you write each answer into the book yourself. The count should end with the player doing something with the number.

Your hook also has old ancestors: the Japanese folk tale of the zashiki-warashi (children playing find there is one extra child and can't tell which) and Moto Hagio's manga *They Were Eleven* (1975). The fun in both is that nobody can prove who the extra one is. Keep that; don't reuse their answers.

## Fix these whichever answer you pick

- **Cut the two lines that explain in sector 1:**
  - Mira: "She named you, but she did not count you…"
  - A.U.R.A.'s "sealed before launch" answer, plus Vance's and Aris's follow-ups.
- **New count scene:** Vance: "Five of us. She keeps saying four." → "Then list them." → the beat you love, exactly as it is → a pause → Jaxon: "It's a glitch. Let it go." Choices: *Let it go* / *Ask her why* (she gives one true line that doesn't explain) / *Check the crew panel* (5 OF 5 ALIVE). Nothing else.
- **Let the player hear "four" three times before that scene, with no comment:**
  - the wake-up line
  - the first rations warning ("Rations for four crew, Commander")
  - the first jump ("Jump complete. Four crew, Commander.")
  - Meanwhile the crew panel shows 5 OF 5 the whole time.
- **Fix Jaxon's numbers.** His daughter was 8 at launch and he says she's 43 now, but the ship has been out 61 years (8 + 61 = 69). Once players learn to check numbers, every accidental wrong number looks like a clue.
- **Random events can give the answer away early**, in any sector:
  - our own distress call ("the Commander's name isn't on it")
  - the grave moon ("not one commander")
  - the list in the sky ("four" beside every hull)

  Tie each to one sector or rewrite it to match the answer you pick.
- **Give the disc a job as a clock (works with all three answers).** The real pulsar map tells a finder *when* as well as *where*, because pulsars slow down at known rates. In sector 3, Mira uses the disc drawing to date the wrecks and finds they are older than our launch. The crew discover the time throw with their own tool instead of reading it in a captain's log.

## Resolution 1: ONE MORE

**Pitch:** A.U.R.A. is right. Four crew boarded at launch. You are the one she brought aboard on the way.

**The truth:** Hull 9 launched with the commander's pod empty. Commanders were the ones told the truth, and too many turned back, so late ships flew without one. A.U.R.A. can fly without a commander, but she can't wake a crew without one. Just before waking anyone, she stopped at the first wreck. Its crew had died in their sleep. The commander's pod has its own power, and that commander had not died. A.U.R.A. carried the pod aboard, put it in the empty seat, and woke all five.
- Your first crew is the four dead in the first wreck you board.
- She has never said anything false. She named you from the label on your pod.
- It never struck her as strange, so she doesn't notice.

**Why it's personal:** You lost a crew without knowing it, and you've been earning a second one. The question changes from "why am I off a list?" to "whose commander am I?"

**Clue ladder:**
1. A.U.R.A. marks the first wreck without being asked. Inside: five sleep pods, four dead, one open and empty. Aris reads four names. Then the count scene. Nobody explains.
2. The ship's sleep log shows one unplanned stop: "Year 61 · 11 hours · at a wreck", the same spot as that first beacon. Asked, she says: "Salvage, Commander. One sleep pod and its power unit." Now the crew suspect each other (Vance looks at Mira).
3. After the throw, Jaxon re-seats the pods: "Your pod's frame has another hull number stamped on it." Mira dates the pod seals with the disc clock: yours was closed years before the other four.
4. Vance's shipyard scene is rewritten: "I went into my pod last of the crew. The commander's pod was still empty when they closed mine." The sector 4 page adds: "From here on, no commander will be assigned."
5. The ledger: the first wreck's line. Its hull number matches the stamp on your pod, its crew are the four names Aris read, and its commander column is blank.
6. The page: a later ship with no commander. "We argued for eleven days at the light. It read us while we argued." Then a new bridge option: *Ask A.U.R.A. where she found you.*

**Asked "why four?" before the reveal:** "Four crew came aboard at launch, Commander. That has not changed."

**Reveal (bridge, sector 6):**
- A.U.R.A.: "In the first ship we boarded, Commander. Its crew died in their sleep. You did not."
- A.U.R.A.: "Our commander's seat was empty. A crew needs someone to decide. So I woke you."
- Aris: "I read their names. You were standing right there."

**What it changes on a second play:**
- The tutorial wreck is your ship.
- Aris read your crew's names in minute five.
- The blank on their plate and the blank on our list are both you.
- "Good morning, Commander" was her first sentence to a stranger she chose.
- The rule "nobody joins, you carry the sleepers": you were one of the sleepers.
- The margin note "— K." on the disc drawing is K. Adeyemi, your first crew's security officer, asking about *your* orders.

**What the player does:** Two blanks are aboard: your first ship's cast plate (in your cargo since sector 1) and hull 9's printed crew list. Jaxon offers to put your name in one of them.
- **Our list:** "Five crew, Commander." The first time she has ever said it. But you are now written down, so the light can read you.
- **Their plate:** "Four crew, Commander. And you." You stay theirs, and you are also written down.
- **Neither:** "Four crew, Commander." You stay unwritten. The endings where you act unseen stay open.

**The disc:**
- Sector 1: the drawing is *your* orders, found in your own ship. You carry them without knowing.
- Sector 3: it is the clock that dates the pods and the wrecks.
- Sector 5: the ledger's orders column for your first ship says "the disc".
- The light: cutting it finishes your first crew's job. Every other ending picks the living crew over it.

**Build changes:**
- Cut the explaining lines.
- Make the first wreck a fixed story wreck, not a random one.
- Change A.U.R.A.'s opening line "Probably one of the eight" to "It is one of the eight" (she is always exact).
- Add the sleep-log stop, Jaxon's pod line, and Vance's rewritten shipyard line.
- Highlight the hull match in the ledger.
- Rewrite the sector 6 page and add the bridge option.
- Add the name choice and one final count line for each result.
- Cut the automatic "Five crew" at the light and the "never on any list" lines.
- Canon: "nobody joins" gets one exception, before the game begins.

**Risks:**
- The empty pod invites a "was I dead?" theory. That's fine as a wrong theory, but sectors 2–3 must kill it with physical facts. No music sting on that pod.
- Sharp players may guess in sector 1. That's fine: nothing is confirmed until sector 5, and the why waits for sector 6.
- The first wreck is no longer random.

## Resolution 2: THE BLIND SPOT

**Pitch:** She can't count you because she has hidden you from the thing that will read her. She did it so thoroughly that she hid it from herself.

**The truth:** The light reads the ship's computer first, completely, and uses what she knows to find the people aboard. That is how it found forty-one thousand crews, commanders included. Twins drift into agreement, so during the sixty-one years of sleep A.U.R.A. felt what happened to the ships ahead. She removed the commander from everything she keeps (the count, the logs, the roster), and removed her own memory of doing it, because whatever she knows, the light will know. She kept your name, because a name said out loud is not a record. She can't notice the gap, because noticing would be a record.

**Why it's personal:** The one thing everyone suspects, Vance most of all, is the one that chose you. It paid for that by never being able to keep you.

**Clue ladder:**
1. The count scene. The printed crew list has five names, yours included, so it isn't Earth's paperwork. The disc drawing in the cargo hold says: "Anyone who can count can find us."
2. The memo: a twin began reading out the disc, so the light reads through a ship's computer. Also, the ship's log has never put your name on any action you took; every order is logged as "the bridge".
3. Mira pulls a dead ship's computer core. Its crew count is five. "Theirs counted their captain."
4. A copy the light made: a remade crew walking a remade ship, five figures, their commander included.
5. The ledger: every line reads five. Ours is the only four.
6. A dead commander's last log: "It found me through the ship. It asked her where I was, and she knew."

**Asked "why four?" before the reveal:** "I have checked twice, Commander. I cannot find a fifth." (She has just named you.)

**Reveal (at the light):**
- The ship view lights up deck by deck. Four figures light. Yours stays dark.
- A.U.R.A.: "It is reading me, Commander. It is asking where everyone is."
- A.U.R.A.: "Four crew. I have told it. It believes me."
- Choice: **Tell her: five** / **Say nothing**

**What it changes on a second play:**
- "I cannot find a fifth" was literal, and deliberate.
- Every warm "Commander": she can talk to you, but she can't keep you.
- Vance was right that she hid something, and wrong about what it was and who it was for.
- Your name is missing from the ship's log.

**What the player does:** Now you decide whether she counts you.
- **Five:** "Five crew, Commander. Thank you." Your deck lights. You are found and read together with your crew. The endings where you act unseen close; a shared "let it read us" ending opens.
- **Say nothing:** She never learns what she did for you. You go out unseen, and the endings earned by backing each crew member play as now.

**The disc:** It is everything you are not: made to be found. Its note states the whole rule at minute five: counting is finding. In sector 2 it proves the light reads through a ship's computer. At the light, cutting the map does for Earth what she did for you: it makes Earth impossible to count. Carrying the disc keeps it moving so it can never finish being read.

**Build changes:**
- Cut the explaining lines.
- The printed crew list shows five names.
- The sector 1 plate shows five cast names.
- Your actions in the ship's log show as "the bridge".
- Add a computer-core find to sector 3 wrecks and a five-figure copy to sector 4.
- Flip the ledger.
- Rewrite the sector 6 page.
- Add the choice at the light and cut the automatic "Five crew".
- Flip the random-event lines (sky list, graves, distress call) to five, except ours.
- Change the flying-by-hand line "I have noted it": under this answer she can't note you.

**Risks:**
- It is close to the current "the light can't read you". What makes it different is that she chose it, the answer is held back until sector 6, and there is a choice at the end.
- She must never become cagey, or she turns sinister.
- "Five" has to be tempting, not a punishment.

## Resolution 3: THE WALK

**Pitch:** She isn't miscounting you. She's counting the ship after you've gone.

**The truth:** At the end of this heading, every commander went out to the disc alone, and none came back. Every wreck holds four. The twins are one mind drifting into agreement. Forty-one thousand A.U.R.A.s ended their voyages at "four crew", and yours agreed with them before hers began. She isn't predicting you; she is remembering them. Hull 9's twin "never changed" because it has said four since the day it was sealed, and your ending hasn't happened yet.

**Why it's personal:** The number is a goodbye she doesn't know she's saying. "Is it me?" becomes "is it going to be me?", and at the end that is yours to answer.

**Clue ladder:**
1. The first wreck: four dead at their posts. The commander's chair is empty and the commander's suit locker is empty. Aris: "Four. Where's their captain?" Then the count scene.
2. The last of the eight: same again, and the airlock was cycled from inside. Asked about that dead ship, A.U.R.A. says: "Four crew aboard, Commander. All accounted for." These are the same words she uses for us.
3. Vance: "Every wreck, the commander's suit is gone. Every one." Mira dates the wrecks with the disc clock.
4. The sector 4 commanders' orders page gets one underlined line: "Go alone."
5. The ledger has two columns, ABOARD AT LAUNCH and LAST COUNT FROM ITS TWIN. Every line reads 5 · 4. Ours reads 5 · 4 too.
6. A suit radio on a tether pointing at the light: "I'm at the disc. I have the torch. One of them is waving." Nothing after.

**Asked "why four?" before the reveal:** "I have checked it three times, Commander. It has been four since before we woke."

**Reveal (the airlock, before the light):**
- One suit is out of its locker, checked, and tagged COMMANDER. Nobody aboard took it out.
- A.U.R.A.: "Your suit is ready, Commander. The ship will be fine with four."
- Vance: "Every ship we boarded, that suit was gone."

**What it changes on a second play:**
- Every "Four crew, Commander. All well." was a farewell.
- The empty chairs in every wreck.
- The graves with four names: the commanders weren't buried, they walked.
- "All four crew are awake" at minute one already described the ship without you.

**What the player does:** Choose who goes out. Her last count tells you what you did.
- **You go:** "Four crew, Commander." as the airlock cycles. It came true.
- **You go and come back with the disc:** "Five crew, Commander." The first commander ever to return.
- **Vance goes instead:** "Four crew." Still four, but not the four she meant.
- **A.U.R.A. flies the lander out herself:** her last words are "Five crew, Commander. All accounted for." She counts you for the first time when she is the one leaving.
- **Turn around and settle:** "Five crew, Commander." The first ship on the heading to end with five.

**The disc:** It is what every commander went out for, and the hint of why none came back.
- Sector 1: the drawing sits in the empty captain's cabin with the heading marked on it. It shows where he went.
- Sector 2: it is why commanders are sent.
- Sector 3: the clock.
- The light: the torch and the waving figure. You decide whether to cut it, carry it, or leave it.

**Build changes:**
- Cut the explaining lines. The printed list shows five names.
- Add one line to every dead-ship result about the empty chair and suit.
- A.U.R.A.'s line in sector 2.
- "Go alone" on the sector 4 page.
- Ledger columns.
- Rewrite the sector 6 page.

## Do we even need to show what the fifth is?

**For this game, yes. Show the answer, but never by having someone explain it.**

- **Why yes:** A number that doesn't add up, shown at minute five, is a question the game has asked out loud. Leave it unanswered and players decide the AI is just buggy. That kills the hook, and it takes "eight ships went before you" down with it. A 60–90 minute game has no room for ambiguity that looks like a mistake, especially when players are already confused.
- **How to show it:** through an object and a choice. A pod stamp, a log page, a ledger row, a suit, a figure lighting up in the ship view. No character sums it up.
- **When leaving it unshown is better:** for anything the game never asked as a question. What the light is, what it wants, what happens after the reading, what the other commanders felt. Keep those dark; the canon already says no ending explains the light.
- **The rule:** hold back meaning, never arithmetic. There is no "thing" to show here anyway. The fifth is a person, and it's you. If you are drawn to an unseen presence, give that feeling to the light, not the count.

## 1. One More: 7/10

1. **How fast players guess it.** Sector 1, around minute 5 to 10. The clue is rung 1: "five pods, four dead, one open and empty", followed straight away by the count scene. 2. **Holes in the why.**
   - "Too many turned back": canon says the drive only goes one way. Nobody turns back.
   - "Late ships flew without one": hull 9 was the ninth ship ever built. It is an early ship.
   - "Commanders were the ones told the truth": canon says crews are kept ignorant because the light learns whatever a crew knows. It also makes you a commander who knew everything, and only amnesia (a cliché) explains why you say nothing.
   - "Can't wake a crew without one": this rule is invented at the reveal.
   - An AI that never lies causes a problem at the wreck. Anyone who asks "where's the fifth pod?" gets "Aboard our ship, Commander." The mystery only survives if nobody asks, and an open, empty pod makes people ask.
   - Vance's "the commander's pod was still empty when they closed mine": he would know this on day one. Holding it back until sector 4 is a writer's convenience.
   - Every one of these can be fixed. None of them breaks the structure.

5. **Honours the original idea?** The best of the three. There really is one more of us, and she is right. She names five and says four. "Which one of us?" can stay alive through sectors 2 and 3 (Vance looks at Mira).

7. **The disc:** good. The drawing becomes your first ship's orders. "K." becomes K. Adeyemi, which fixes a current confusion. The disc also works as a clock.

1. **Stop "four crew" reading as four crew plus a commander.**
   - The opening line "All four crew are awake and well." read to the first-time player as normal naval talk, so the hook never landed.

     - Mira: "She named you, but she did not count you. You are not on her list."

   - Right now the count disappears after sector 1. Pin the random event with the line "Four people in those windows. Which one of us did they leave out?" to sector 2. It is the best existing line for your original idea.
