# Assessment, 2026-09-29: story vs great indie writing, add-or-polish, itch.io readiness

Three independent readers, none of whom edited anything. The short version is in [WHERE_WE_ARE.md](WHERE_WE_ARE.md).

**Verdict in one line:** finish, do not extend; the count decision is the blocker; the mechanical fixes are about two days; the music licence must be checked before anything goes on itch.

## 1. Story: a narrative editor's pass

**Silent Exodus, a narrative editor's pass (2026-09-29)**

### 1. Grade

C+. The premise is an A: one heading, forty-one thousand ships, and a kind voice that says eight. The script is a C because it explains what it should show, says its best facts twice, and lets a random card in sector 1 hand the player sector 5.

What it most resembles: Solaris and Moon inside FTL's body. That is a gift, because anyone who has played FTL already knows how to play this, and nobody has put a Solaris ocean at the end of an FTL corridor. It is a problem because FTL's events do not remember each other, and a mystery is nothing but memory. The story beats arrive in the same three-choice, plus-15-salvage container as the salvage, so a player reads "It has not read me. I don't think it can find me." with the same eye they read "+25 Salvage. Aris +1 Stress."

### 2. The story lines

**The count.** How the good ones do it: the player notices, and nobody explains. Obra Dinn never says who did it; you write it in the book yourself. Portal never lets GLaDOS admit she is wrong; the walls do it for her. 2001 spends an hour telling you HAL has never made a mistake so that one wrong prediction is terrifying. The wrong number appears in routine lines, several times, before anyone reacts, and when they react they do not solve it.

Ours opens and closes the mystery in one scene. Vance: "There are five of us on this ship. She keeps saying four." (the player has heard it once). Then Mira: "She named you, but she did not count you. You are not on her list." Then A.U.R.A.: "The crew list was sealed on Earth before launch, Commander. It has four names on it. I cannot change it." Then Aris: "Somebody wrote this list before they knew who would be flying the ship." Four explanations in ninety seconds, all paperwork. The plate closes it again: "Four names and a blank line. Same as our crew list." Meanwhile the best count line in the game is a stress-2 bark most players will never see: Mira, "A.U.R.A. sets out four meal trays. I always put out a fifth."

Fix. Cut the four explaining lines and the plate line. Keep the exchange up to "And you, Commander. Four crew." and Jaxon's "It is a glitch." If the player asks why, she says something true that explains nothing: "Four crew, Commander. I have checked." Put "four" in two routine lines before the scene (the rations warning already says "Four crew eat a great deal"; add the first jump: "Jump complete. Four crew, Commander."). Promote the meal-tray line to a fixed sector 2 moment.

On the answer: your objects have already voted. The list in the sky ("beside every one of them, the number four"), the graves ("Thousands of stones, and not one commander"), the hollow asteroid ("with a 4 beside each"), the memorial stone ("four names and a blank line"), our own distress call ("Four crew. The Commander's name isn't on it."), the copy of our ship ("Four people in those windows. Which one of us did they leave out?"), the door ("four people are walking away. One of them turns and waves."), the colony coda ("The tracks led toward where it rises."). Every one says the same thing: every ship ended with four, and the commander is the one who went. That is The Walk (The Script is the same shape), and it costs the least because the clues are built. Add an empty chair and an empty suit locker to the two fixed wrecks in sectors 1 and 2, "Go alone" on the sector 4 page, a LAST COUNT column on the ledger, and make the finale the act of choosing who goes out. One More is a good twist for a character with an inner life; Cora has none by design, so "who am I" has nothing to land on.

**Eight versus forty-one thousand.** How the good ones do it: the number is counted by the player, not spoken by a character. Obra Dinn's whole game is a fixed ledger. Sunless Sea almost never gives a number; the dread is in the description. When a number is given it is the same number every time, and it only rises.

Ours contradicts itself inside a sector. Sector 1's jump: "Five beacons ahead, Commander, all earlier Exodus ships." Sector 2's arrival: "The last of the eight ships should be out here." The tape says "hundreds", the throw says "Thousands of them", Mira says "Nine hundred", Vance says "Three hundred and eleven". Aris "reads out eleven names" from one wreck. Jaxon's daughter was "eight when we left. She'd be forty-three now", on a ship "61 YEARS OUT FROM EARTH", said by a man who is "forty-two". In a game about one wrong number, every accidental wrong number is either a clue or a bug, and the player cannot tell which. SILENT SHIP has "five people sit at their stations"; THE RING OF SHIPS has "Four name tags lie on the table." Right now that difference is accidental. Under The Walk it is the clue: five means the commander stayed, four means he went.

Worse, the random pools leak the ending. THE LIST IN THE SKY ("hull numbers climbing past forty thousand"), THE ARCHIVE ("Forty thousand ships, and every entry ends with the same word: stopped."), THE STOREHOUSE ("Thousands of ships, and we were told eight."), the buoy ("Thousands of them, in order."), and THE FLIGHT RECORDER's full decode, which recites the sector 3 page word for word ("You were sent less far back in time. The drive sends every ship into the past"). All are "any sector" or sectors 1 to 5. A player can draw forty thousand in sector 1 and the climb is over.

Fix. One ladder, and gate to it. Sectors 1 and 2 may not show a number above 31 (Vance's hull). Sector 3 hundreds, 4 thousands, 5 tens of thousands. Move every card with a bigger number to the sector where that number belongs, the way the graves are already held to sector 5. Fix Jaxon to "She'd be older than I am now." Cut the recorder's time-travel branch.

**The time throw.** How the good ones do it: Outer Wilds does state its hardest fact flatly, but only after you have watched the sun explode ten times; the sentence confirms, it never informs. Heaven's Vault makes dating the thing you do.

Ours states it before the question is felt. The sector 3 arrival card says "Their numbers are higher than ours, and they are a hundred years old." and the objective line says "One of their captains wrote down why." before the player opens a wreck. The page then says "It said we were not sent further than the others. We were sent less far back in time." and the ship's sceptic replies "Back in time? That would explain the numbers." The good version of the beat is already in Aris's sector 3 moment: "We're ship number nine. A ship with a higher number should be newer than us, not older." That is the question. And the ledger's "On every line, the ship was lost before it was launched." is the answer as an object, with no physics.

Fix. Cut the log sentence and Jaxon's reply. Keep Aris's question. Let the disc be the clock (below) and the ledger's two date columns be the only explanation. Nobody says "back in time" out loud; A.U.R.A., asked, gives two dates and no theory. The throw dream stays; it is the best five minutes in the game. Cut "SECTOR 3 Generated." (reads as debug) and "The third burn did not finish." (the player has counted two).

**The disc.** How the good ones do it: an object the player uses, not a card they close. Obra Dinn's watch, Heaven's Vault's inscriptions, Outer Wilds' signalscope.

Ours is a card the ship tells you to ignore ("aura — An old curiosity, Commander. I would not spend time on it."), with a button that says "FOLD IT AWAY" and a note that says "it stays in your cargo". It reappears at the light with the same four notes, unchanged. Between sector 2 and the light it does nothing. Its facts are mixed: "Thrown out of our solar system in 1977" is the Voyager record; "One of them is waving" is the Pioneer plaque. And "— K." is Kael Vance to half the players and K. Adeyemi to the other half.

Fix. Three jobs. Sector 1: found in the empty captain's cabin with our heading drawn on it, so it shows where he went. Sector 3: a clock. The fourteen lines on the real map encode pulsar periods, and pulsars slow at known rates, so the map says when as well as where. One card: Mira lays the drawing over a wreck's star fix and dates it older than our launch. The crew find the throw with their own tool. Sector 6: the thing in your hand; the ending is what you do with it. Pick one probe (1977, drop the waving figure) and sign the margin with a hull number, not an initial.

**The light.** How the good ones do it: never an adjective, only a verb. Solaris never explains the ocean; it shows copies that are slightly wrong (your Wrong Place ending's "got the grass wrong" is that line). Sunless Sea's Dawn Machine is described only through what it does to people who look at it. Outer Wilds' last ten minutes have almost no words. SOMA shows what being copied costs before it asks you to choose.

Ours has two voices. The right one is the facts panel: "It looks like a sun" / "It is not" / "Nothing answers. Something reads." and the probe: "It was not destroyed. It was read." The wrong one is the old build: "It defies comprehension. It defies physics. It waits.", "AGE BEFORE TIME", "THREAT LEVEL ???", "It defies description. It defies physics. It defies sanity.", "THRESHOLD: For a moment, you see it. THE STRUCTURE. It sees you back.", and A.U.R.A.'s "All predictive algorithms return null. Proceed with... I do not know."

And it has no teeth. The sector 6 page tells the player what will happen ("It has not read me. I don't think it can find me.") so the reading at the finale confirms a memo instead of happening. Nothing is taken from anyone.

Fix. Delete every old-voice line and write the NavView panel and threshold events in the facts-panel voice (keep "Age: [ERROR - NEGATIVE VALUE DETECTED]"; that is the throw in one gauge). Give the reading teeth with one line, the Signalis move, a line that comes back slightly wrong: on arrival in sector 6, A.U.R.A. says, verbatim, "Good morning, Commander. All four crew are awake and well." Nobody comments. That is the reading beginning, it costs fifteen minutes, and it pays off the opening. At the finale, let the light quote the crew back before the choice (Jaxon's letters, Aris's list in her own voice), which is currently buried inside the Break the Map ending text.

**A.U.R.A. and the twins.** How the good ones do it: institutions stay in the walls. Portal never explains Aperture in a memo; SOMA explains the ARK once, through a person who wants something. The twins are a fine idea delivered as the game's most memo-shaped memo, then repeated as a coda on all six endings: "In the vault on Earth: The twin of our ship's computer goes quiet. Nobody on Earth knows that this means it worked." and "Twin 0009 begins, very quietly, to repeat itself."

The memo's one great line is "Keep building ships." It answers why in three words, and nothing after it should explain what it explains. The sector 4 page then does, and breaks canon doing it: "One ship turned back." (nothing can).

Fix. Cut all six vault codas. Keep the memo at four lines ending on "Keep building ships." Answer Mira's "Does ours?" once, exactly, never again: "Yes, Mira. It has not changed since launch." Change "turned back" to "tried to turn back."

**The crew's wants and endings.** How the good ones do it: one want per person, a small arc, a definite end. Citizen Sleeper does this for every character. 80 Days and Roadwarden keep a tally you never see and show it only in what people say to you. Your standing system is that, and it works. The twelve crew moments are the best text in the game.

What falls short is the payoff. Six paragraphs of narration: "You go out alone, into the light. The disc is there, gold, the size of a dinner plate. You cut through the map on it". The player reads what they did. The ending card prints "BREAK_MAP" as its kicker. Mira's ending punishes the player who trusted the ship ("A.U.R.A. turns the ship into the light and burns the drive, the disc and herself with it.") and it is unclear whether anyone lives.

Fix. Make all six endings answers to one question, who goes out to the disc, and end every one on A.U.R.A.'s count, as the options doc already proposes. You go: "Four crew, Commander." Vance goes with you: still four, not the four she meant. Settle: "Five crew, Commander." Mira's ending, fixed: A.U.R.A. flies the lander out with the disc herself, and her last transmission is "Five crew, Commander. All accounted for.", the first time she counts you, when she is the one leaving. Give the choice stakes: if the light has read her and she says five, it has counted you, and going out costs something. The reprises you have are right (Jaxon finishes the letter, Aris reads the names, Vance hands over the torch); keep them and cut each paragraph to three lines.

### 3. A.U.R.A.

GLaDOS is confidently wrong and the walls correct her; the joke is her authority. GERTY is warm, truthful and loyal to the man, not the company; the horror of Moon is that the machine is good. In Other Waters puts you inside the AI, and every line the human says to you is a reading with a feeling under it, never more than two lines. Subnautica's PDA is dry, exact, withholds by policy ("Are you certain whatever you're doing is worth it?") and never lies. The canon says A.U.R.A. is GERTY. The script has her doing GLaDOS without the jokes.

Every A.U.R.A. line must do four things. It is true and would survive the player checking it; the count is the one exception and must stay the only one. It reports and never interprets; she gives the reading, the crew give the meaning. It is short, addressed to a person, with no ellipsis and no adjective. It costs her nothing to say; she is never hurt, proud, cagey or afraid, because the moment she has moods the count becomes a mood.

The model: "Space is very large, Specialist." "Four plates per table is standard issue, Commander. That part is correct." "I have no record of a ship numbered 3,306, Commander. We are ship nine." "I've logged the source as a star." "It was not destroyed. It was read." And "Thank you, Commander." Then, after a long time: "What do I do now?"

What breaks it, nearly all in 09-ship-and-crew.md, and most of it fires on the player's first orbit: "Stable orbit. Data is compiling. Shall I draft epitaphs or mission briefs?" "Results are... results." "We arrive. You consume. The pattern continues." "Another step away from accountability." "The void suits you, Commander." "Logged. Filed. Forgotten. That's how you treat them, isn't it?" "Success. Even stopped clocks are right occasionally." The premonitions: "We are expected here. I don't know how I know that. But I do." The mutiny: "This is not murder. This is optimization." The Wrong Place: "It is... improbable. But existence here is impossible. We must try." The colony ending where she shuts off the air: "Settling here is not in my orders, Commander. Please return to the ship." Every ellipsis in her mouth is the tell.

Fix. Replace the four-tier commentary table (about 150 lines) with one tier of about twelve, one per event, in the model voice. Cut the premonitions, the air vent and DIGITAL MUTINY. Keep ON OUR OWN ("Her reports are correct and short.") and the cooperative coda "Four crew, Commander. The colony is well.", which is perfect. Let trust show only in whether she flies for you; "You let me fly again. Good. You should rest more. I have us." already does this. On "Usually, Commander. Not always." after the ridge: keep it. Seventy percent is a probability, not an error, and it is the one time she is humble.

### 4. Information order

Current, as a first-time player meets it:

- Opening: five woke; she says four. Eight went first; none called home.
- S1 wreck: a drawing of a gold disc in a colony ship's orders; a note from "K."
- S1 bridge: she names five, says four; it is explained (list sealed before a commander was picked).
- S1 plate: every ship has four names and a blank; explained again.
- S1 Vance: he saw more than nine built; hull 31.
- S1 Aris: none died of anything she can diagnose; no name above eight.
- S1 jump: five beacons ahead, in a line.
- Any sector, at random: forty thousand hulls; every entry "stopped"; the commander missing from our own call; a ring pointing at a light; the drive sends ships into the past.
- S2 tape: hundreds on our heading; "Old recordings degrade"; nobody calls her on it.
- S2 memo: twin computers; a twin recited the disc; something has it; keep building ships.
- The throw: every ship in one line; ours last; a pinhead of light.
- S3 arrival card: higher numbers, a hundred years old; a captain wrote down why.
- S3 page: sent less far back in time.
- S3 Aris: higher number, older ship; how?
- S3 jump: she asks whether to continue if the end of the heading costs crew.
- S4 page: the lie was deliberate; the computer knows and will not tell.
- S4 jump: no way back.
- S5 ledger: lost before launched; commander column empty; we are the last line.
- S5 jump: a light dead ahead.
- S6 page: it read my crew; it cannot find me; you are the last ship; go and decide.
- Finale: she says five; it cannot find you; choose.

The order I would use:

- Opening: five woke; she says four. No narration points at it.
- S1 first wreck (fixed): four dead at their posts; the commander's chair and suit locker empty; the disc drawing in his cabin with our heading drawn on it. Aris reads four names.
- S1 routine: "four crew" in the rations line and the first jump line. The crew panel says five.
- S1 bridge: she names five, says four. Nobody explains. "Four crew, Commander. I have checked."
- S1 Vance: hull 31 on the slipway had our heading painted on it.
- S1 jump: the eight beacons lie in a straight line; she offers to fly it.
- S2 last of the eight (fixed): same again, airlock cycled from inside. The tape: hundreds. "Old recordings degrade." Vance: "Damage doesn't add ships." The memo, four lines, ending "Keep building ships." Mira: "Does ours?" "Yes, Mira."
- The throw, unchanged.
- S3 arrival: hundreds of beacons, all ours; Mira reads numbers. No age, no why.
- S3 page: Mira dates a wreck with the disc; older than our launch. Aris asks how. A.U.R.A. gives two dates and no theory.
- S4 page: orders for commanders only; "Do not tell your crew"; one new underlined line: "At the end of the heading, go out to it alone."
- S4 jump: her "do I continue?" question, now that the player knows who wrote it into her, and "no way back", on one card.
- S5 ledger: launched and lost as two columns; four crew and a blank on every line; LAST COUNT reading 4 on every line and blank on ours. The graves, already held to sector 5: four names on every stone.
- S5 jump: the light.
- S6 arrival: "Good morning, Commander. All four crew are awake and well." Nobody comments.
- S6 page: not a log, a suit radio on a tether: "I'm at the disc. One of them is waving." Nothing after.
- Finale: she is read first and says "Five crew, Commander." It has counted you. Choose who goes out. Her last count closes it.

Why: one question per sector, each sharpening the last. Why four; how many really; how can they be older; who lied and why; where did every commander go; who goes out. The count stops being answered by paperwork and starts being answered by chairs. Time travel is never spoken. The disc is used three times. "Keep building ships" is the last thing said about Earth. Nothing above 31 can appear before sector 3, so the climb is felt. And the light's first act is a line the player heard at minute one.

### 5. Length and density

Six pages and about twenty scripted moments for 60 to 90 minutes is right. Obra Dinn has sixty fates and no speeches; Firewatch is four hours and most of it is walking. The fixed text is not the problem. The random text is: seven Exodus wrecks, six derelicts, six stations, five colony ruins, nine strange places, six asteroid fields, six distress calls, nine ship problems, three late places, thirty-seven surface finds, a bark table, and a colony-ending generator. Roughly ninety cards with three choices each. A 90-minute run sees maybe eighteen. The pool is oversupplied five to one, the story facts are scattered across it at random, and the register slides back to the old build the moment the fixed text stops.

Cut, in this order:

- The four-tier A.U.R.A. commentary: 150 lines to 12.
- The colony warning modal: 40 lines like "Toxicity levels are 400% above survivable limits. The math doesn't work." to one line per planet type.
- The probe's thematic messages: 80 lines like "Piezoelectric crystals yielded a modest charge." to one line per outcome.
- The EVA result lines by planet type ("Hazmat protocols successful.") to three generic lines.
- In 08-settling.md, every paragraph belonging to systems the canon deleted or the player never meets: symbiotes, cyborgs, neural links, HIVE_MIND, "nanofiber hull plating", "Commander [surname] kept strict discipline". Keep the generator; cut it to the lines in the current voice.
- The Wrong Place escape ("The ship screams. Reality screams louder.") to three lines. Its ending text is good; keep that.
- The game-over screens ("The void claims another Exodus.") to the plain voice.
- The three coach lines that state a page's contents before it is found: "One of their captains wrote down why.", "Earth wrote orders for commanders only. A copy is in one of these wrecks.", "One wreck here carries a list of every ship Earth ever built." All three become "Search the wrecks."

And add one silence: after every found page, nothing random fires until the next stop. Sector 6 already does it ("Nothing here is random any more."). Do it for all six. Duskers is dread by absence; the pages need air around them.

7. Rewrite the light's old-voice text (NavView panel, threshold events, "defies sanity") in the facts-panel voice. One hour.
8. Cut the six vault codas; end every ending on her count line. One hour.
9. The disc as clock: one sector 3 card where Mira dates a wreck by the map; cut "sent less far back in time" and "That would explain the numbers." Two hours.

### Extra gameplay

No. Nothing new. The game already carries an ethics system, a mutiny, a revival mechanic, symbiote and cyborg tags, a pocket dimension, a fabricator, three mini-games and a boarding game, and the story is still delivered through cards the player closes. The one thing the story is missing is not a system; it is a found page that does something, and you already have the model: the ledger prints the hulls you boarded in gold. That is Obra Dinn's book. The disc-as-clock is the same move. The only addition I would allow is one card: Aris's list, viewable from the cargo hold, growing through the run, shown once before the light. The game tracks it already ("[name] adds a name to her list") and has never shown it. Everything else is cutting. You are right that it is a polishing job; the polish is subtraction.

### Is it itch.io-ready

Not this week. Mechanically yes: the loop, the throw, the docking and the boarding are real. Narratively, the first thing a new player meets on their first orbit is the worst text in the game ("Shall I draft epitaphs or mission briefs?"), the hook dies at minute five, and a random card can hand them the ending in sector 1. Before a public build:

- Merge PR #5. The public link is still the 20 September game.
- Items 1 to 8 above. About a day and a half of text.
- The ending card printing "BREAK_MAP" as its kicker.
- The title screen's "BUILT WITH AI ASSISTANCE // 2024": itch asks for AI-generated content to be disclosed on the project page, so put it in that field, and the year is wrong.
- Decide the count, or ship with it open and say so in the devlog. Shipping the sealed-list answer means shipping a tagline whose promise closes at minute five.

Free with tips is the right release; be honest about what it earns. A free browser narrative game on itch tips in the tens of dollars unless a streamer or a site picks it up. What it reliably earns is ratings, playtesters and a devlog audience, and ratings are what itch's browse pages sort by. So: pay-what-you-want with a suggested $3 (people tip more when a number is suggested); put the tip ask on the ending card, not the title screen, because nobody tips before they have felt something, and ask for a rating in the same sentence. The page needs a 20-second GIF (the throw or the docking), "60 to 90 minutes, in your browser, no install", and two sentences: the tagline you have, then "A friendly voice says eight. The wrecks say forty thousand." Post it as a playtest build first, take three weeks of comments, then post a 1.0 devlog, because itch surfaces updates. Six endings gated by standing already give a reason for a second run; say so on the page.

## 2. Gameplay: a systems designer plays it

**How I played.** Server was down; I started the project's own `star-map` launch config, played a fresh save at `?art=dither&mute=1` for about twenty minutes: all of sector 1 (two stops, the count scene, the disc, the strange site, the jump) and the first stop of sector 2 (the sleepers wreck, the uncut tape). No station or asteroid field spawned in either sector, so docking and boarding are judged from code plus the spawn odds. My tools cannot hold a key, so I flew one lander by tapping (hovered fine) and handed the rest to A.U.R.A.; the lander verdict below is from code and one auto landing. End state: energy 27, rations 7, salvage 90, 3 sleepers, standing Vance 2 / Aris 2 / Mira 3 / Jaxon 0, pages found 0 of 6.

### 1. The loop, and which decisions are real

As a player: you get a map with three to five dots and two or three stops. You click a dot, maybe pay 2 energy for a one-word hint, warp (a timing bar), and a card interrupts you with a crew moment. In orbit you press DEEP SCAN, the panel fills in, you press SEND TEAM, pick two faces, watch a lander come down, and read a card with two or three options that show their price in chips (+25 salvage, -1 ration, 30% chance). You pick, fold away a page, BREAK ORBIT, do it again, then JUMP SECTOR (another timing bar, a talk card, an arrival card). Six times. Then a light and a menu of endings.

Per stop the player is asked eight things and about two are decisions. Real: which dot (but the information is a tag like "Signals: TECH, WRECKAGE" bought for 2 energy, and the sector's story page sits unmarked on one wreck, so this is mostly a lottery; I left sector 1 without its page); and the card's options, which trade resources against respect and standing. Noise: whether to deep scan (always yes, 2 energy), whether to tune it by hand (a small link under the big button; I never triggered it by accident and the reward is +1 data, which does nothing on the main path), probe versus team (the site is the content, so team), who goes (perks only matter inside a station; on the ground it is faces), fly the lander or let her (auto lands soft every time; manual has no upside), plot the burn or let her (auto is always "fair", manual risks 25% extra for a 15-30% refund of a 12-energy warp). Per sector: which two or three of the dots (real, under-informed), the campfire choice (real, small), when to jump (when the counter hits 0), and the fabricator (salvage 90 after four stops; upgrades cost 90-250, so perhaps one per run).

### 2. Should anything be added?

No new systems. Fourteen systems and five minigames for a seventy-minute game is already two games' worth, and the designer's instinct that more would overcomplicate is right. What is missing is not a mechanic but a consequence: nothing the story hands you is ever used. I checked every read of `exodusLogsFound`, `DISC_DRAWING`, `BRIEFING_TAPE` and `_colonyKnowledge`: pages print as "PAGES FOUND n / 6" on the end screen, the disc and tape are cargo items you can reread, and data only feeds the colony rating formula and two colony sentences. A player who finds all six pages and one who finds none get the identical finale card. In a game whose spine is "objects contradict the lie", the objects are stickers.

Three candidates, in the order I would build them:

- **Show the site on the map, free** (2-3 h). Nodes with a wreck, station or strange site carry their tag on the map label from arrival, and the sector's page wreck reads "one of ours, transponder still on". Fixes the one real per-stop decision (where to go) and stops the story pages being luck. Fold the long-range scan into this and delete its energy cost.
- **Pages gate what the finale says, not what it offers** (6-8 h). Keep all six choices always visible, but each choice's description and A.U.R.A.'s line at the light draw only on what you carry: without the memo, "Destroy the map" reads "Go out to it. Vance thinks there is something in there"; with the ledger, Vance can say what he learned. Knowledge becomes progression the Outer Wilds way, and a second run has a reason to exist. Build after the count is decided, because the count decides what the pages say.
- **The disc as a clock** (4-6 h): one use-from-cargo action on sector 3+ wreck cards, "Date this wreck with the disc", giving the age against the hull number. The crew discover the time throw with the tool in their hands instead of reading it in a captain's log. Only worth it if the time throw is kept.

I would build the first now and the second once the count is settled. Not the third until then.

### 3. Cut or fold

Pulling weight: the map with scarce stops; the encounter cards with priced options; standing as the ending key; rations as the clock; energy as the price of a stop; the found pages, disc and tape as objects (once they gate something); the boarding game, whose visible air costs and walk-back reserve make it the best-designed thing in the build; the lander as a twenty-second mood piece.

Noise, and what to do with it:

- **Long-range scan.** 2 energy for "TECH". Fold into free map labels.
- **The tuning minigame.** Opt-in behind small print, rewards a stat that does nothing. Cut it; DEEP SCAN is one button.
- **Data / colony knowledge.** Mine hit 5 in sector 1 ("Tech Mira read the whole list. -1 Ration, +4 Data"), after which the HUD number changes nothing. Remove it from the HUD; if the colony endings want it, count pages instead.
- **Probes.** A parallel path to EVA with resource text and no story ("Piezoelectric crystals yielded a modest charge"). Cut, or keep only as the gas-giant option where nobody can land.
- **Stress traits, breakdowns, mutiny, sedation, confinement.** A second game bolted on. With quarters rest on every jump stress rarely reaches 3, so most players never see it and the ones who do get "Vance has a gun on you". Keep stress as a 0-3 mood chip on cards; cut the rest.
- **A.U.R.A. ethics tiers, premonitions, the air vent, Jaxon's override, the tech-fragment reset.** Canon says she is never eerie; the 797-line commentary bank says "Shall I draft epitaphs or mission briefs?" and "Something waits for us at the next destination. I can feel it in the signal patterns." Both played in my run. Cut the bank to a dozen calm lines.
- **The colony attempt with S/A/B/C rating, "Symbiotes", "Cyborgs", "Touched".** Leftovers of cut systems in the flight-recorder card. Fold settling into one plain ending text.
- **Docking.** Well built, but the station spawn is 20% in sector 1 rising 5% a sector: about two stations per run, and a 60% chance of seeing none before sector 3. Either guarantee one station in sector 2 and one in sector 4, or auto-dock and keep only the boarding.
- **Fabricator and upgrades.** Seven items at 90-250 salvage in a run that earns about 25 a stop. Fold the useful three (sensors, autodoc, cargo racks) into finds or campfire choices.
- **Reliance meter, asteroid dig-style choice, ship-malfunction pool.** Each is one line of value; fold into existing cards.

### 4. Difficulty and pacing, from the numbers

Stops: `MIN_STOPS_PER_SECTOR = 2, MAX = 3`, stops = places minus one, so about sixteen stops and five jumps a run. Energy: start 100; warp 10-19 (stations 4-7), refund on arrival 19-60% of the warp (`WARP_REFUND_SCALE 0.75`); scan 2; deep scan 2; team 5; jump 20 (40 with engineering down); a bad plot adds 25%. My run, playing the way the coach bar tells you to (scan everything, send the team, take the humane option): 100 at start, 55 after two sector-1 stops, 35 after the jump, 45 after the campfire bribe, 27 after one sector-2 stop. That is a player entering sector 3 with about seven energy and one stop left. Rations: 20 of 30 to start, one per warp, jump and team trip, plus the story options charge food ("Read the names, -1 Ration", "Take the minute, -1 Ration", "Carry all three pods, -3 Rations"). I was at 7 after four stops. At 0, "Crew can survive 2 more actions without eating", then a death each action. Gains are rare (fourteen call sites, most +2 or +3, mostly in stations and asteroid fields you may never see). Verdict: a first-time player who reads the story options is punished for it and is close to starving by sector 3. Stranding proper is guarded (`checkStranded` waives it if a probe, an unscanned planet or any usable item exists), so the real killer is food, and it arrives before the story does.

The ending gate is the opposite of what the designer fears. `STANDING_NEEDED = 2`; call sites per person are Jaxon 9, Vance 12, Aris 14, Mira 12, and three per person are near-guaranteed (the count scene, two crew scenes at 75% a warp, plus a campfire every jump). After 1.3 sectors I held Vance 2, Aris 2, Mira 3. Most first-timers will reach the light with three of the four personal endings open, plus "Take the disc and keep flying" which is never locked, plus the sleepers' vote if they carried pods. The lock screen will usually show one lock, Jaxon's. The actual problem is that standing is granted for options people pick for the chips: "Let A.U.R.A. fly the line, +10 Energy" is Mira's moment, so siding with someone is invisible and the finale's "you backed them" line reads as an accounting note rather than a relationship.

Method: I read the docs and the full player-facing script, checked the actual runtime files, ran the game in a browser at 1280×720 and at phone size (your server on :8000 was down, so I ran my own static server on that port for the session; it stops when I finish), and checked the music and portrait files' metadata. I did not edit anything.

### What I found in the build

**Loading and packaging.** `index.html` loads 6 stylesheets and 61 script tags with `?v=` cache-busters, all relative paths, no `fetch`, no ES modules, no absolute paths. That is exactly what itch's iframe on html.itch.zone wants; the cache-busters are harmless there. Runtime weight: about 1.5 MB of JS and 130 KB of CSS, 12 MB of portraits in the repo (10 files, 1024×1024, ~1.2 MB each, but only 5 are used and they render at 28–64 px), and 8.3 MB of music (a 93-second loop that plays on first click, and a 4:36 finale track loaded only at the light). `tools/package_itch.py` already does the right thing: copies only what `index.html` loads, shrinks the 5 used portraits to 256 px if Pillow is installed, and stamps `<html data-release>` so `theme.css` hides the orange TEST cheat button. But `dist/silent-exodus-itch.zip` is from 20 September and is missing 17 scripts the current `index.html` needs (DockingGame, LanderGame, TheThrow, FoundPage, StoryReel, SceneArt and more). Uploading it as-is would fail on the first click.

**Saves.** One key, `silentExodus_save`. `saveGame`, `loadGame`, `getSaveInfo`, Coach, DitherCore and AudioSystem all wrap storage in try/catch, so a browser that blocks third-party storage still runs the game (you just cannot save). Autosave fires after each warp, each sector jump, each boarding and one more action. A hard refresh mid-lander or mid-docking goes back to the title with CONTINUE and resumes at the last autosave (the map, in orbit at the last place you warped to); the current visit is lost but nothing breaks. The one gap: the game-over screen's button is `onclick="localStorage.removeItem('silentExodus_save'); location.reload()"` with no try/catch, so in a storage-blocked browser "REBOOT SIMULATION" does nothing and the player is stuck. Erase-save on the title is a two-click arm ("ERASE SAVE AND START OVER?", disarms after four seconds) and works. Two Safari facts to know: cross-site iframe storage is partitioned and can be purged by tracking prevention, so some Safari players will lose long saves. itch's own thread on this says storage is tied to the html.itch.zone domain and there is no way around it beyond export/import or a server (https://itch.io/t/1504924/revisiting-the-storage-question-for-html5-games).

**Title screen.** Loads fast, looks good at both sizes. The browser tab says `PROJECT STAR MAP` (the `<title>` in index.html) while the screen says SILENT EXODUS. Tagline: "EIGHT WENT BEFORE YOU. NONE OF THEM CALLED HOME." (fine; CANON says the old spoiler tagline is gone, and it is). Bottom line: "BUILT WITH AI ASSISTANCE // 2024", faint, wrong year, and vaguer than the itch disclosure you will have to make anyway. The header shows TEST, ART: DITHER and AUDIO buttons; TEST is only hidden by the packager's flag, so never upload a hand-made zip.

**Input.** Lander (left/right/A/D/space) and docking (arrows/WASD) both have on-screen hold buttons wired with `pointerdown`, and docking has an AUTO option. Everything else is mouse. Keyboard handlers `preventDefault` on their keys, so arrows will not scroll the itch page once the iframe has focus. Fine for itch.

**1280×720:** the whole layout fits with no scrollbars. **Phone:** unplayable. The grid is fixed at `280px 1fr var(--panel-width)`; at 375 px the map column collapses to nothing, the right panel is squeezed beside the ship, the page overflows sideways, and the coach tip renders one word per line. The only narrow-width rule is `@media (max-width: 1100px)` which shrinks fonts. Mark the project not mobile-friendly and say so on the page.

**Console and network:** zero errors, zero 404s on load and through the opening to the map.

**Legal and credits.** There is no LICENSE, no credits screen, no README at the root, no font files (Orbitron and Share Tech Mono come from Google Fonts, both OFL, no licence file needed but the page phones Google). The music files carry ID3 comments: `Heaven.mp3`: "made with suno; created=2026-02-01"; `Space_Project_Background.mp3`: "made with suno; created=2026-01-28". So both tracks are Suno, not only the ending. The crew portraits carry a Midjourney prompt in their PNG text chunk ("...32bit pixel art, hand-crafted look, elegant and serene --raw --v 7 Job ID: ...") and an IPTC `DigitalSourceType` of `trainedAlgorithmicMedia`. So every image and every piece of music in the game is generative, and the files say so to anyone who looks.

### 3. Free plus tips, honestly

How itch's money works for you: "$0 or Donate" (pay what you want, minimum 0) shows a suggested amount at download with a "No thanks, just take me to the downloads" link (https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing). Plain "No payments" shows nothing. So "free" and "pay what you want with $0 minimum" differ only in whether the prompt exists; nobody is ever gated. The prompt appears at download, not at play, which is why a browser-only game gets almost no tips: players never pass through the moment where itch asks. One developer in itch's own forums reports browser games "never get any donations, even if they have the button enabled" (https://itch.io/t/676052/are-donations-to-free-games-rare). Forum consensus for free downloadable games is 1 to 3 percent of downloaders paying (https://itch.io/t/3021842/are-there-people-who-actually-pay-when-you-put-pay-what-you-want). The one well-documented case, Hive Time, a full management game with press coverage: over 40,000 downloads, under 600 payments in about ten months (roughly 1.5 percent), average payment around $9 (https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2020/10/developer-of-hive-time-reflects-on-the-release-and-their-pay-what-you-want-model/, 24 October 2020; the developer's summary at https://itch.io/t/1081038/money-for-the-honey-a-case-study-of-pay-what-you-want-pricing-on-itchio, 26 November 2020). His two findings that matter to you: an in-game prompt explaining pay-what-you-want with an opt-out "had a positive impact", and being described as pay-what-you-want rather than free-to-play correlated with purchases; front-page features raised downloads but not payments. Third-party guides put the average tip on free content around $3 to $4 (https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/how-to-make-money-on-itchio-indie-game-guide); treat that as a rule of thumb, not data. itch takes 10 percent by default plus processor fees of roughly 30 cents and 2.9 percent per payment (https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments).

What that means for a 60 to 90 minute, text-forward browser game from a first-time developer with no following: a launch post in the right places gets you roughly 300 to 1,500 plays in month one; without posts, tens. Tips: 0 to about 15 payments, $0 to $60. If a streamer or a YouTuber with a "one-hour space mystery" audience picks it up, $100 to $300 and a few hundred comments. Your BUSINESS_PLAN's "€30-50" month one is the optimistic end of that. The money is not the point; the comments, the ratings and a finished game on a page with your name on it are the point, and they carry into whatever you release next.

What I would do for this game:

- Pricing: "$0 or Donate", suggested $3. Revenue share: leave the default.
- Ship both a browser embed and a downloadable zip of the same build. The zip is free to make (the packager already produces it), gives the itch app and offline players something, and, more importantly, creates the download moment where itch actually asks for money. The audio code already uses HTML Audio for `file://`, so test the zip once from disk and say "playable in browser or download" on the page.
- The one-line tip ask on the ending card, as above.
- Devlog: publish the launch post as a devlog the same hour (devlogs feed itch's browse and your followers). A second post a week later on how the endings and standing work (spoiler-marked). A third when the story update lands. Communities for launch day: itch's Release Announcements forum, r/WebGames (this is exactly their game), r/playmygame, r/IndieGaming with the GIF, the intfiction.org announcements board (text-forward games are welcome there), Bluesky and Mastodon with #indiedev and the GIF. Reply to every comment for the first two weeks. Post once each; do not repost.
- Do not run a paid minimum. It would cut plays by ten to fifty times and defeat the reason to be in the browser.
