# The disc story, in plain words (2026-10-10)

Your structure from today, made into one story, in two versions: with a copy among the crew, and without. Both full
versions are in `docs/spines/disc-copy.md` and `docs/spines/disc-nocopy.md`. Both were checked for plot holes and repaired.
Nothing in the game was changed.

## 1. Your structure, in five lines

1. Ships after hull 9 had a better drive that throws you further back in time, so they landed before us and lie here as
   wrecks. **Filled in:** the new drive throws only once. They were stranded where they landed, lived out their lives and
   died there. That is why there are wrecks everywhere in sector 3, with graves beside them.
2. Ships 1–8 left before us and died on the way; we are the ones who reach. **Filled in:** the old drive hops sector by
   sector, and it is the only drive that can keep going. We are the last ship that has it. In sector 2 the player also
   avoids, by hand, the mistake that killed hull 8, so "why us" is earned, not luck.
3. Earth's secret is the real gold disc, and later ships carried a video showing what it looks like and ordering crews
   to fetch it. **Filled in:** how the disc got out here. Earth flew it out itself, proudly, on its hundredth birthday in
   2077, on hull **1,977**. The video starts on the next ship, **1,978**. Earth changed its mind because of the 2029 call,
   whose last line was always "DO NOT SEND THE D—". Nobody knew what the D meant until the disc had flown.
4. Those crews caught the disc and built a station on a planet to send it home, and it failed. We find the disc, then a
   log saying it must go home and nothing else may have it. **Changed:** "they lost it" became "it never left". The
   station's screen said SENT, the rack never opened, and the crews died believing it. If they had truly lost it, nobody
   could find it, us included.
5. The light deceives the AI way (sure of itself and wrong) and wants to know exactly where Earth is, maybe because ship
   computers are like it. **Filled in:** why it cannot just read the address out of the 41,207 ship computers it has
   already met. Every ship flew the same heading, and no ship can measure how far its throw took it. So every computer
   holds the same line back to Earth, and no point on it. The disc's star map is the only point.

**The 2029 call** is the station's own call home, sent from about the year 1700 and arriving in 2029. It is the one
loop in the story. On screen it is only two papers that match: hull 8's broken recording, and the station's whole tape.

## 4. My recommendation: A, the copy, and the copy is Mira

- It answers the best hook in the game with substance. A.U.R.A. names five and says four, and she was right. That is the
  kind of answer you asked for in September, instead of "the computer is broken" or "you are missing from a list".
- It is your own idea ("it created one of the crew, a copy since the moment we teleported"), and it is foreshadowed the way
  you asked: the twin with our faces shows the copy before anyone says it.
- **Is it too cliché, and does it break our idea?** It does not break it; it finishes it. It would be a cliché if the crew
  hunted an impostor or Mira turned evil. Neither happens. She never knows, never plots, and the player works it out from
  objects one sector before the crew does.
- **Why Mira.** She slept linked to A.U.R.A., so the light read her fully along with the machine. She trusts A.U.R.A. like a
  friend, and the machine she trusts was the only witness. Not Cora (the player is never the copy), not Vance (we need the
  doubter), not Jaxon (his wish to stop fights the light), not Aris (her list is how we mourn Mira).
- **Fair clues, all objects:** the bandaged hand in the launch film; the dry pod; "Your stitches are gone." "I've never had
  stitches."; MAIN STRET, "No, there isn't."; the station's sure launch officer; Mira moving in the twin's window.

## 5. The ending (version A)

At the light, Vance asks A.U.R.A. which pod she does not count. "Pod five. It was open and empty when I woke you." Mira
understands first: "It's me." Aris writes Mira's name on her list of the dead while Mira watches. A crew moment follows,
with "Not yet" offered first. The question is: **where does the disc go?**

- **Burn the map** (always open; Vance hands you the torch). You burn the map off the disc by hand. **Lost:** the only hello
  we ever sent, and the way home, forever. Earth is safe and never knows. Mira stays, knowing what she is. Every morning
  A.U.R.A. says "Four crew"; Vance answers "Five".
- **Send it home** (Mira's ending; you need to have backed her). You use the tether, and Mira carries the disc across to
  the twin and goes with it. The twin turns for Earth calling itself EXODUS-9. **Lost:** Mira, and maybe Earth: nobody
  knows whether it is a hello or the light. A.U.R.A.: "Four crew." Vance: "Four."
- **Stop short** (Jaxon's ending; the settle council). You land on the dark side of Tethys-1 Prime, where the light never
  falls. **Lost:** the ship never flies again, and they keep the disc in the dark for the rest of their lives, two hundred
  years before they were born. Jaxon writes his letter. "Four crew." Jaxon: "Five. Count again tomorrow."

Version B has the same three, except that A.U.R.A. opens the airlock in the second one ("Hand it over"). No ending explains
the light, and no ending goes home. After every ending the launch report plays again.

## 6. Decisions for you

1. **Copy or no copy?** My answer: the copy, Mira (version A).
2. **The station never sent it (SENT over a full rack), instead of losing it?** My answer: yes. It keeps your failed
   station, and it is the clearest picture of the light's AI-style lie the game has.
3. **Hull 1,977 carries the disc, and the video starts on 1,978?** My answer: yes. It is the number you asked me to pick,
   and the player can put 1977 and 1978 together.
4. **The new drive throws only once, so its crews were stranded where they landed?** My answer: yes. One rule explains the
   wrecks everywhere, the graves, why the station crews could not carry the disc anywhere, and why we can.
5. **Build three endings now: Burn the map, Send it home, Stop short?** My answer: yes. Each one takes something by hand
   after a crew moment, and each one needs the disc.
