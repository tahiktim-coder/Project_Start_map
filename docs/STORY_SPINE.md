# The story spine: the message that started everything (proposed 2026-10-02)

**Status:** the designer's idea, with refinements suggested by Claude. Not yet canon. Once agreed, it replaces CANON.md §2 (why
ships are thrown), §5 (why Earth keeps building), §6 (the count) and §9 (the ending).

## The designer's idea

A bootstrap paradox. Long after us, an Exodus crew worked out everything and sent a warning back to Earth, back in time:
**"Send ZERO Exodus here. Don't send anyone to [coordinates]."** Only fragments arrived. Earth read them as an instruction:
*send Exodus ships to this place.* The arrival of a message from the future was so enormous that governments rebuilt
themselves around it. Earth had already launched the gold disc in 1977; another fragment said the disc should never have
been sent. So Earth built Exodus 1, then 2, then kept going. The warning caused the very thing it warned against.

We are the ninth. Eight launched before us. None reached the place. We find their wrecks.

## Suggested refinements

1. **We are the ones who send it.** If a ship a hundred thousand hulls later sends the message, the player only watches.
   Hull 9 arrives last of all (every later hull was thrown further back in time and lands ahead of us), so we are the only
   crew who can see the whole graveyard. We work it out. We send it. The loop closes on the player's own hands.
2. **The wire is A.U.R.A.'s twin.** Every ship's computer has a twin in a vault on Earth; the link carries *state, not words*
   and *does not care about when* (already canon). That is why the warning arrives in pieces: it was never words to begin with.
   It is also how the light reads its way back toward Earth: **every ship is a wire home.** That is what "send zero" means.
3. **Hull 9's twin "has never changed"** because we have not sent the message yet. The moment the player sends it, it changes
   for the first time. Earth's vault has been waiting for us.
4. **The count falls out of it.** The broken message carried a header: *EXODUS-9 · FOUR CREW.* Earth built hull 9 for four.
   A.U.R.A. reads her crew count down the same timeless link, so she reports the ship **as it will be when the message is
   sent**: four aboard. Five are aboard now. One of us will not be aboard at the end, and A.U.R.A. has known it since minute one.
   The commander walks out to the disc (the walk out) to send it from there. "Four crew, Commander" was always a goodbye.
5. **The disc's role.** Its map says *where* Earth is. The warning said do not send it; it was already gone. Earth read the
   fragment as "the disc is there" and made reaching it the mission. At the end the disc is both the thing the light is reading
   and the antenna strong enough to reach home.

## The disc: what it holds, and how it got there (added 2026-10-03, proposed)

**What the disc actually holds.** The real 1977 Voyager record's pulsar map holds two things and only two: *where* Earth is
(fourteen lines from the Sun to fourteen pulsars) and *when* the disc was made (each pulsar's period, and pulsars slow down at
known rates). It holds nothing about where to send ships. The coordinates came from the broken message, never from the disc.

**The hole this fixes.** Voyager travels about 17 km a second. In 2026 it is a fraction of a light-day from the Sun. It cannot
be at the end of the heading. So the disc in the light is not Voyager's.

**The fix: every Exodus carries a copy.** The broken message said, among other things, *do not send the disc.* What reached
Earth read as *send the disc.* So Earth bolted a gold copy of the record into the hold of every Exodus ship. Every ship that
reached the light delivered Earth's address to it. The thing at the end did not find us. We kept mailing it our map.

- The drawing in the first wreck's logbook is that ship's own cargo note for its copy. "Why is this in a colony ship's orders?"
- **Our ship carries one too.** After the first wreck, the crew opens a sealed crate in our hold and finds our own gold copy.
- The finale's disc is our own copy, or the first one that ever reached the light.
- What we send home at the end: send zero ships, and no disc.

**What the dating minigame really does.** Our copy is the ruler. A wreck's navigation computer stored its last star fix: the
pulsar periods and angles it measured on the day it died. Lay that fix over our disc. How far the periods have slowed tells
you *when* it died; how far the angles have swung tells you *where*. Knowing exactly when and where the wreck stopped,
A.U.R.A. can retrace its last course and find where its crew went: that is the story planet the minigame reveals.

## What the player does at the end

At the disc, the commander composes the message: a few words chosen from what the run taught them. Whatever they choose,
the game shows what will survive the link: *SEND … EXODUS … [coordinates].* The loop holds. One possible exception, earned
by the hardest path (all crew backed, every page found, every wreck dated): the message arrives whole. That is the only ending
where Earth stops building ships, and the only one where A.U.R.A. says "Five crew, Commander."
