# Story core: what to keep, and the one story I recommend (2026-10-10)

You asked which of our ideas are good, and for one big idea before any details. This page answers that. The three full
stories are in `docs/spines/` (`reader.md`, `loop.md`, `unknowable.md`). Each one was hunted for plot holes and repaired;
the repairs are listed at the end of each file. The yardstick is `docs/STORY_CRAFT.md`, and every past idea is in
`docs/STORY_IDEA_BANK.md`. Nothing in the game was changed.

## 1. Our past ideas, in ten lines

1. **Keep** the time rule: hulls 1–9 have the old drive, the new drive starts at hull 10, so a higher number means an older wreck.
2. **Keep** the light that reads and copies, with three rules that never break. Its nature stays unknown on screen.
3. **Keep** your deceiver: the light wants to know where Earth is. It gives the light a want and gives being read a cost.
4. **Keep** the one disc and the dating. Its star map is the only way home, and the player rebuilds it by hand.
5. **Keep** your idea that A.U.R.A. changes after the jump. She stops counting the one person who can switch her off.
6. **Keep** the 2029 call in the disc's language, sent by a copy of us. That is Earth's strange willingness.
7. **Keep** the launch report, the dark jump into the sea of ships, the twin in sector 5, and "We have history".
8. **Drop** the disc at the light, copies of the disc, the second message to fetch it, and the light that stops ships.
9. **Drop** the vault twins, "every direction", the commanders' orders memo and the six old endings.
10. **Drop** the sealed-list leftovers, the robot crew, the seven-sins system and the pursuer.

## 2. The three stories

### A. The Reader (your deceiver idea)
1. The light reads whatever its light falls on and looks like a warm home star. From 41,207 crews it learned everything
   about us except the way home, because Earth wiped the way back from every ship's chart.
2. In sector 5 it copies our ship, and the copy's call home, in pieces, is the message Earth heard in 2029.
3. We rebuild the way home by dating wrecks with the disc's map, and at the light A.U.R.A. needs one last date from the
   commander's key.

- **1** We find the eight ships, **but** A.U.R.A. counts four, and hull 6's computer did the same. **Therefore** Vance makes us check her: we start dating.
- **2** Hull 8's crew printed where the route came from: a 2029 call, and Earth's order that no chart keeps the way home. **Therefore** we date every wreck to rebuild it.
- **3** The jump goes dark into a sea of ships, **but** EXODUS-31, built after us, is fifty years dead. **Therefore** we need our own date.
- **4** The green worlds are copies, and their crews forgot their homes (Ines's street). **Therefore** nobody lands. Mira: "By the stars it's eighteen-something."
- **5** A twin of our ship, four still figures in its windows, calls home in A.U.R.A.'s voice, word for word the 2029 page. **Therefore** go on, or stop.
- **6** At the light, A.U.R.A. is one date from the way home and the crew start to forget. **Therefore** the commander chooses.

### B. The Loop (your "the future started it" idea)
1. In 2029 Earth heard a call in the disc's language, signed EXODUS, counting 41,000 ships and ending "Four crew, all
   accounted for", and built the programme it described.
2. Every jump throws ships back in time, so the light met all 41,000 in reverse order, and we arrive last.
3. The call is ours: at the light our computer calls home, our voices are lost on the way, and "four" is true because one
   of us went into the light.

- **1** Hull 6's dead computer says "four crew" over a plate with five names. It is not just us.
- **2** Hull 8's sealed envelope holds the 2029 call. It ends on A.U.R.A.'s exact line.
- **3** The dark jump into the sea of ships. EXODUS-31, built after us, is fifty years dead.
- **4** On copied worlds, hull 2,210's settlers forgot home, then their own names.
- **5** Our twin flies beside us with four still figures in its windows. The beacon count heads for 41,000.
- **6** Our sky dates to the 1840s, so a call from here lands in 2029. Someone must go out so the others get away.

### C. The Unknowable (your "keep it unknown" idea)
1. The light reads everything near it and makes copies, and on every ship that reached it, it took the nearest person
   first: the commander, alone in the bow seat.
2. Earth followed a broken 2029 message with a route, an impossible date and the number 41,207, and A.U.R.A. says "four"
   because every computer near the light drifts toward how every ship ended.
3. At the light you choose who sits nearest: you, someone else, or nobody, and then all five slowly forget home.

- **1** Hull 6's log: five aboard, and its computer said four after the first jump.
- **2** The 2029 tape gives a route, a date older than the disc and 41,207. Earth's note: "Tell crews the route. Never the date."
- **3** The dark jump into the sea of ships. Hull 31 is a century dead. A dead channel says our words.
- **4** Hull 2,210's settlers copy out their names each morning, with more blanks each week.
- **5** Our twin: four still figures, and the commander's bow window dark.
- **6** The graves at Tethys have four names on every stone. It takes the nearest. Choose who that is.

**Why the Reader.** It is the only one where all your leads become one answer: the light is the deceiver, it wants home,
Earth's strange willingness is its doing (through our copy's call), Earth's secret is part of the main quest, and the AI
changes near the light. It is also the only one where the disc changes the ending: the chart you build by hand, wreck by
wreck, is the thing the light wants. Its weak point is that there is more to hold, so only the count, the key, the chart,
the twin and the forgetting go on screen. Everything else stays in the writers' notes.

**The grafts.** From the Loop: the launch footage plays again after every ending, and now the player sees what it meant.
From the Unknowable: the light never pulls ("I've set our course for the light, Commander"), and on screen nobody ever says
what the light is.

**The ending.** A.U.R.A. flies us in. The crew start to forget: Jaxon cannot picture his daughter's face, and Mira loses her
mother's name. She asks: "One more, Commander. The sky here. Then I'll have the way home, and I'll take us out." There is a
crew moment, with "Not yet" first.
- **Finish the map** (always open, done with the dating). She counts you at last, "Five crew, Commander", because your key no
  longer matters. The crew keep what they still have, and the twin turns for home. It costs Earth, and you know it.
- **Cut her out** (Vance hands you the torch). Your key opens her core. You lose A.U.R.A., Mira's friend, and the chart. The
  ship drifts free, and you fly by hand. Her last words: "Four crew, Comm…" Vance: "Five."
- **Stop short** (Jaxon, at Tethys-1 Prime, the settle council). You keep the key, so the map never closes. They forget slowly.
  Jaxon writes his letter while he still can.
- *Later, optional:* **Aris goes out** on the tether, to be read first so the others keep their homes longer.

**What we lose by picking it.** Choosing to send the message: here it is fate, carried by the sector 5 crew moment. The
Loop's "four was our gravestone all along". The Unknowable's pure mystery: the light's want is now shown. The sleepers' vote,
and every old ending built around the disc.

## 4. What this answers

- **The light.** It reads whatever its light falls on. The nearer, the faster: machines in days, people over months. It
  takes a person's home first. It can copy what it has fully read. It gives no heat, never moves and never speaks. It
  wants home. What it is stays unknown.
- **Earth's secret.** The 2029 call, and the order Earth took from one fragment of it, "nobody calls home": every chart
  wipes the way back. Earth hid both. That is why the light never found Earth in 41,207 ships.
- **The 2029 message.** It is our twin's call from sector 5, made from our crew's own recorded words. It arrived in pieces
  because the copy was unfinished. Every earlier copy's call landed before anyone was listening.
- **The disc.** There is one disc, launched in 1977, and it is never out here. Its star map sits in every ship's navigation.
  Dating wrecks with it rebuilds the way home, and that is both the crew's hope and the light's want.
- **Four crew.** "Five crew aboard" on the launch pad, then "four" after the first jump. Near the light she starts to want
  what it wants, and the first sign is that she stops counting the commander, who holds the key that switches her off.
- **The twin.** The light's copy of our ship, made from A.U.R.A. as she thinks. It turns a quarter of a second late. In its
  windows are four still figures and an empty pilot's seat. It can call home, but it cannot find home. Sector 4's undatable
  copy of hull 31 prepares it.
- **Lost time.** Every jump throws us further back. We arrive last, in about 1850 by Earth's calendar. On screen that is two
  lines: Mira's "eighteen-something" and Jaxon's "She isn't born yet."

- **Cut:** "every direction", the blank fifth panel, the empty commander column, "not one commander", "It cannot find you",

## 6. Decisions for you

1. **Is the Reader the story?** My answer: yes, with the two grafts. Every later step waits on this one.
2. **Is the 2029 message fate rather than a choice?** My answer: yes. The twin sends it in every run, and the sector 5
   crew moment carries the weight. Making it a choice at the light breaks the one timeline.
3. **Does the count leave out only the commander, and follow deaths?** My answer: yes. This replaces "frozen at four", so it
   always points at you and your key.
4. **Which endings get built?** My answer: three now (Finish the map, Cut her out, Stop short). Build Aris going out later.
5. **Is our "now" about 1850, so Jaxon's daughter is not born yet?** My answer: yes, but only two lines on screen. The ledger
   years and the dating chart get retuned to match.
6. **The sleepers: cut them, or keep them as cargo?** My answer: keep them, minus the vote. A sleeper who cannot remember
   where they are from is the first cost of being read the player sees.
