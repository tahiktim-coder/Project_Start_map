# How the game talks (2026-09-24)

The rule above every other rule: **a player who has never read the canon understands every line the first
time they read it.** People in this game talk the way people talk in a grounded sci-fi film — *Alien*,
*Moon*, *The Expanse*, *Interstellar*. They say what they mean. They react like people. Nobody speaks in
riddles, and nobody performs a character quirk.

## Hard rules

1. **One clear idea per line.** Complete sentences. Contractions are fine. 20 words at most.
2. **No pet names, no tics, no catchphrases.** Nobody names the reactor, the lander, a rock or a crate.
   Nobody narrates like a nature film. Nobody counts things out loud as a habit. No line or idea is used
   twice in the game.
3. **No riddles.** If a line needs the reader to decode it, rewrite it. Say the thing.
4. **No invented jargon.** Use words a player already knows. "Distress call", not "hail". "Hull", not
   "ribs". "Lander", not a nickname. "The computer", "A.U.R.A." or "the ship", not "the kettle".
5. **Numbers are said plainly.** "Hull number 980", not "nine-eighty". "About a hundred years old", not
   "a century of rust".
6. **Plain metaphors only, and rarely.** "It looks like a sun" is fine. "Three rations of cold" is not.
7. **Explaining is allowed** when a person would naturally say it. "How can a ship with a higher number
   than ours be older than us?" is a good line. The one thing nobody says outright until the pages show it:
   *why* (the drive throws ships back in time).
8. **Dead people do not speak.** Check before any line is spoken.
9. **A.U.R.A. is calm, polite and exact.** She calls the player "Commander". She is never eerie, never
   sinister, never glitchy. Every number she gives is right, except one: she always says there are four crew.

## The crew

Character comes from **what they want** and **how they react**, not from a gimmick.

| | who | what they want | how they talk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jaxon Mercer**, engineer, 42 | tired, warm, practical. Left a daughter on Earth. | to stop somewhere good and live | plain, a bit dry. Talks about keeping the ship running, and about settling down. |
| **Kael Vance**, security, 45 | blunt, careful, suspicious of A.U.R.A. Worked at the shipyard before the launch and saw more ships being built than anyone told them about. | to find out the truth, and be believed | short, direct, often the one asking the uncomfortable question. |
| **Aris Novak**, doctor, 38 | the kindest person aboard. Keeps a written list of every dead crew they find. | to understand what happened to them, and treat the dead with respect | gentle, serious. |
| **Mira Chen**, scientist, 29 | young, curious, excited by what she finds. Talks to A.U.R.A. like a friend. | to be told what to do; she trusts the ship | quick, enthusiastic, then scared when it gets strange. |
| **A.U.R.A.** | the ship's computer | to keep the crew moving | calm, exact, "Commander". |
| **Cora Moon** (the player) | the commander | — | never speaks. |

## Checks before anything ships

- Read the line out loud. Would an actor say it without asking what it means?
- Does it repeat a line or idea used elsewhere in the game?
- Is the speaker alive at this point?
- Does every choice say plainly what you get and what it costs?
