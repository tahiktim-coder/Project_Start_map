# Spine: The Disc, with nobody copied aboard (2026-10-10, repaired)

A proposal, not canon. It is the designer's own structure from 2026-10-10, made into one simple story, with no copy among
the crew. The light's copying shows only in the twin ship and the places it makes. It shares its backbone (the call, the
drives, the disc, the video, the station) with `disc-copy.md`. Nothing in the game was changed.

## In three sentences

1. A broken call in 2029 ("DO NOT SEND THE D—") made Earth send ships down one heading; in 2077 Earth proudly flew the
   real gold disc, its address, out after them on hull 1,977, and only then understood the D.
2. Every ship after ours had a drive that throws once, deep and far back in time, so they all landed before us and died
   where they landed; the video ships caught the disc and built a launcher to throw it home, and near the light its screen
   said SENT while the rack never opened; we, the last ship that can still hop, find the disc in the rack.
3. Near the light, a computer answers one question with what it expects instead of what is there, calmly; A.U.R.A. counts
   the four people she knows and not the commander, who has never spoken to her, and the light builds its copy of our ship
   out of what she holds, so the twin has four faces and wants the disc.

## The light: three rules that never break

1. **Every jump flash is its light passing through the ship. It reads machines, fully,** and copies what they hold: places,
   ships, faces, voices. A copy looks right from far away and is wrong up close. Nothing grows in a copied world, and the
   figures in a copied ship never move. It learns a thing like the disc only through a machine that has scanned it.
2. **Once its light has passed through a computer, that computer answers one question with what it expects instead of
   what is there,** calmly and with total confidence. That is the AI way, like a machine that miscounts the letters in a
   word and is sure of it.
3. **It never moves, never speaks in its own voice and never takes by force.** What it wants must be handed over.

**What it wants (shown):** the disc. **Why (writers only):** ship computers are the nearest thing to itself it has ever met,
and every one came from one place. It wants to go there. It cannot move; its copies can. It cannot tell its guesses from
facts, and every ship flew the same heading without being able to measure its throw, so every computer holds the same line
home and no point on it. The disc's pulsar map is the only point. On screen, once, in sector 3: a dead computer asks the
boarding team "Where was I made?"

## The answers

- **Why every ship came this way, and what Earth hid.** In 2029 Earth heard a broken call from this heading, signed
  EXODUS: "…SHIPS YOU SEND… HERE… DO NOT SEND THE D—". It built the programme toward it and told crews to find a new world.
  It hid only the call.
- **The call.** The station's call home, sent from about 1700 by Earth's calendar: "This is Exodus. The ships you send land
  here, in the past. Something here wants the disc. Do not send the disc." The only loop; on screen, two papers that match.
- **The disc.** The real Voyager gold record, the only one. In 2077, for its hundredth birthday, it flew again on EXODUS-1977,
  "toward whoever called". On launch day someone at the programme saw the banner and understood the D.
- **The drives.** Hulls 1–9 hop sector by sector with almost no throw back, so they arrive in launch order; ships 1–8 died
  on the way of ordinary mistakes. From hull 10 the new drive throws once, and the stronger it is, the deeper and earlier it
  lands you. Then it is spent. So every later ship landed before us and stayed where it fell. Higher number, older wreck.
- **The video ships and the station.** From hull 1,978, every ship carried Earth's video: "Find EXODUS-1977. Take the disc.
  Keep it in its case. Never let the ship's computer scan it. Bring it home. If you cannot, burn the map." Hulls 1,978 to
  about 2,500 landed in sector 4 a few years before 1,977, built a launcher on Pallas-17 Major, caught the disc and loaded
  the rack. The launcher ran on a ship's computer, read by the flash when that ship arrived. On launch day it said SENT. The rack never opened. They sent
  their call home, lived out their lives there, stranded, and died believing it.
- **Why nobody else found it.** Every ship that knew about the disc landed before it and was stranded. Every ship after it
  (hulls 10–1,976, and 1–8) had never heard of it.
- **Why we reach.** We are the only one of the old nine still flying, and only the old drive keeps hopping. In sector 2 the
  player avoids the mistake that killed hull 8 (it burned its fuel chasing the call). *"They had better drives. Ours still works."*
- **The order that it must go home.** Sector 6, hull 40,963, centuries too early and too deep to reach the station: "If you
  have it, bring it home. If you can't, burn the map. Nothing out here may have it. It says exactly where Earth is."
- **Why A.U.R.A. says "four".** At launch the pad speakers, in her voice: "Five crew aboard." After the first flash she
  answers "how many crew" with the people she holds instead of the people she sees. She holds hours of four: Jaxon's
  letters, Aris's list, Mira's talks with her, Vance's arguments. The commander has never said a word to her. She names
  all five and does not notice. The count does not follow deaths: she still holds the dead.
- **The twin.** The light's copy of our ship, built out of A.U.R.A. since the first flash: our paint, four of our faces
  still in its windows, the commander's window dark. It comes the jump after we pick up the disc, because the light read
  A.U.R.A., who heard us talk about it. It hails in A.U.R.A.'s voice.

A.U.R.A.: "Their crew count matches ours, Commander. Shall I open the airlock?" A crew moment first, "Not yet" first.

No ending explains the light, and no ending goes home. After every ending the launch footage plays again.

## The opening: the launch report

Crowds, five people on a podium, a reporter. The pad speakers: "Five crew aboard. All accounted for." Asked a question,
Commander Moon only nods: "The commander had no comment." Jaxon: "Ten gets the new drive. We missed it by one." The
reporter: "Nobody at the programme will say what the 2029 call said."

## What changes in the built game

- **Opening:** the launch report replaces StoryReel `program` and the briefing card, and replays after every ending.
- **Crew panel:** a row per person, "Recorded by A.U.R.A.", in hours. The commander's row: nothing.
- **Pages (`ExodusLogs.js`):** hull 6's plate and its ordinary failure; hull 8's recording; the video; the station's log
  and call tape; A.U.R.A.'s printout of the twin's crew list; hull 40,963's log. Cut the vault twins, the commanders' memo,
  "sent less far back", the ledger and "it cannot find me".
- **Places (`StoryPlanets.js`):** sector 3 holds hulls 10–1,976 with graves; Pallas-17 Major becomes the station, with the
  rack, the launcher and the copied street; sector 5 is where the twin comes alongside.
- **Films:** TheThrow goes dark, then wrecks everywhere, close and thick. Corridor 5's twin gets four still faces, a dark
  commander window and the hail.
- **Dating (`DiscDating.js`):** ages only; copies return "no sky". The case stays shut near A.U.R.A.
- **Finale:** cut the disc-in-the-light card and the six old endings; build the three above.
- **Canon fixes:** CANON §2's table, §7's "same moment", §8 (Mira is the tech), PAGE_LIGHT. Then `node tools/export_script.js`.

## Writers only

- **Order of arrival here:** the 40,000s (about 1450), the station crews and hull 1,977 (about 1700), hulls 10–1,976
  (1750–1840), hulls 1–8 (1820–1845), us (1851). The call's wavefront passes sector 2 around 1840.
- **The loop:** the station's call caused the programme that caused the station. Only the disc has a first cause.
- **Hull 6's computer** did not say four: its commander talked to it. That is why our count looks like our fault alone.

**Repairs made in this pass (2026-10-10).** The disc no longer drifts here on its probe (Voyager could never cover the
distance): it flies on hull 1,977, shared with `disc-copy.md`. The station is built by the video ships, not hull 10. The
new drive throws once (wrecks everywhere, graves, why us). Nothing is thrown home: "Aim it home" and the capsule are cut,
and Earth's video gives the fallback, burn the map. The line and the dot added. Hull 8 hears the call itself.
