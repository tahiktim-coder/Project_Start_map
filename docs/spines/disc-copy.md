# Spine: The Disc and the Copy (2026-10-10, repaired)

A proposal, not canon. It is the designer's own structure from 2026-10-10, made simple, with one copy among the crew.
It was hunted for holes against `docs/STORY_CRAFT.md` and repaired; the repairs are listed at the end. Nothing in the game
was changed. The shared backbone (the call, the drives, the disc, the video, the station) is the same in `disc-nocopy.md`.

## In three sentences

1. A broken call in 2029 ("DO NOT SEND THE D—") made Earth send ships down one heading; in 2077 Earth proudly flew the
   real gold disc, its address, out after them on hull 1,977, and only then understood the D.
2. Every ship after ours had a stronger drive that throws a ship deep and far back in time, and only once, so they all
   landed before us and died where they landed; the ones that carried Earth's video caught the disc, built a launcher to
   throw it home, and died believing a screen that said SENT; we, the last ship that can still hop, find it in the rack.
3. The light wants the one thing that says exactly where Earth is, and its copies are sure of themselves and wrong; at our
   first jump it took Mira and gave us back a copy, so A.U.R.A.'s "four crew" was right all along.

## Fixed rules

**The light.** These three rules never break. What it *is* is never said on screen.
1. Every jump flashes white (already built). That flash is its light passing through the ship. It reads minds: machines
   fully, people only in part. Someone joined to a machine is read with it, fully. A thing that is not a mind, like the
   disc, it learns only through a machine that has scanned it.
2. What it has read fully, it can make again. A person read fully does not come back: the copy wakes in their place and
   does not know. Machines and places it copies and leaves where they are. Every copy is sure of itself and wrong about
   small things. That is the AI way: confident and wrong, like a machine that miscounts the letters in a word.
3. It never moves, never speaks and never takes by force. What it wants must be carried to it. It looks like a warm home star.

**The drives.** Hulls 1–9 have the old drive: it hops sector by sector, and the time it throws you back is tiny, so they
arrive in launch order. Ships 1–8 died on the way, each of a different mistake. "Ten gets the new drive": one long throw,
and the stronger the drive, the deeper and further back in time it lands you. Then it is spent. So ships 10 and up landed
before us, stranded where they fell, and a higher number means a deeper, older wreck. Nothing goes home.

**The line and the dot.** Every ship flew the same heading, and no ship can measure how far its throw took it. So every
ship's computer holds the same line back to Earth and no point on it. The disc's pulsar map is the only point.

**The count.** A.U.R.A. counts the people who slept in her pods all the way from Earth. On waking, four pods were frosted
with a living sleeper inside. The fifth, Mira's, was open and dry, and Mira was standing beside it.

## The answers

- **Why every ship came this way, and what Earth hid.** In 2029 Earth heard a broken call from this heading, signed
  EXODUS: "…SHIPS YOU SEND… HERE… DO NOT SEND THE D—". Earth built the programme toward it and told crews to find a new
  world. The only thing it hid was the call, because a call from a ship that did not exist yet sounds mad.
- **The call, in one sentence.** It is the station's call home, sent from about 1700 by Earth's calendar, where the video
  ships had landed: "This is Exodus. The ships you send land here, in the past. Something here wants the disc. Do not send
  the disc." It is the only loop, and on screen it is two papers that match.
- **The disc.** The real Voyager gold record, the only one. For its hundredth birthday in 2077 it flew again, proudly and
  in public, "toward whoever called", on the ship numbered for it, EXODUS-1977. On launch day someone at the programme saw
  the banner and understood the D.
- **The video ships.** Hull 1,978 left a few months later carrying Earth's video: what the disc looks like, its case, and
  "Find EXODUS-1977. Take the disc. Keep it in its case. Bring it home. If you cannot, burn the map." Every later ship
  carried it too, with a better drive "to get there first". So they all got there first.
- **The station.** Hulls 1,978 to about 2,500 landed in sector 4 weeks to a few years before hull 1,977. A few dozen
  crews built a launcher on Pallas-17 Major to throw the disc home, caught hull 1,977 when it fell in, and loaded the rack.
  The launch officer, who slept on the link, said it went. The screen said SENT. The rack never opened. They sent their call
  home, believing it, lived out their lives there, stranded, and died. The disc is still in the rack.
- **Why nobody else found it.** Every ship that knew about the disc landed before it arrived and was stranded. Every ship
  that landed after it (hulls 10–1,976, and 1–8) had never heard of it, and the new-drive ones could not move anyway.
- **Why wrecks everywhere.** The new drive throws once. Hulls 10–1,976 lie all over sector 3, beside the graves of crews who
  lived out their lives a century or more before they were born. Higher number, older wreck, older graves.
- **Why we reach.** We are the only one of the old nine still flying, and the old drive is the only one that can keep
  hopping. In sector 2 the player avoids the mistake that killed hull 8 (it burned its fuel chasing the call). The player
  can say it: *"They had better drives. Ours still works."*
- **The log that says it must go home.** Sector 6, hull 40,963, a day short of the light. It carried the video, landed
  centuries before us, too deep to reach the station: "If you have it, bring it home. If you can't, burn the map. Nothing
  out here may have it. It says exactly where Earth is. Our computer keeps asking where it was made."
- **The light: what it wants, and how it deceives.** It wants the dot. Shown, never said: dead computers near it ask where
  they were made, and the twin comes the moment we hold the disc. It deceives the AI way: it looks like the home star you
  hoped for, and its copies are confident and wrong. *Writers only:* it found minds like its own in our ship computers and
  wants the place where they are made. It cannot move; its copies can. It cannot tell its guesses from facts, and every
  computer it ever read holds the same line. The disc is a fact.
- **The twin.** The light's copy of our ship, made at our first jump, and its way home. It comes when we pick the disc up,
  because at the next jump it reads A.U.R.A., who heard us talk about it. It turns a quarter of a second late.
- **Why A.U.R.A. says four.** She is right, by her own measure: four people slept in her pods all the way from Earth. Hull
  6's log did the same, so the crew blame "a fault in the series". Nobody asks her which pod until the light.

## The copy: Mira

The launch film says she "sleeps linked to the ship". At the first jump's flash the light read A.U.R.A. fully, and Mira
with her. Mira went into the light. The copy woke in her place, early, beside a dry pod.

| who | verdict |
|---|---|
| Cora (you) | No. The player is never the copy (craft rule 8). |
| Vance | No. "The accuser was the thing" is *The Thing*, and we need him as the one who doubts. |
| Jaxon | His want (to stop) fights the light's want (to go on). The copy would be out of character from minute one. |
| Aris | Nothing ties her to machines, and her list of the dead is better used to mourn Mira. |
| **Mira** | **Yes.** She slept linked to A.U.R.A., trusts her like a friend, and wants to be told what to do. The light got in through the one person joined to its kind of mind. The machine she trusts was the only witness. |

**Fair clues.** Objects or reactions, never a speech. Three agree before the twin.
- The launch film: Mira's left hand is bandaged, and the reporter says she "sleeps linked to the ship".
- Waking: four pods are frosted. Mira is already up, and her pod is dry. Jaxon: "Always first, Mira."
- Sector 1: Aris: "Your stitches are gone. Not even a scar." Mira: "I've never had stitches."
- Sector 4: the station log: "The one who slept on the link said it went. She was always sure."
- Sector 4: a copied street's sign reads MAIN STRET. Vance: "There's an E missing." Mira: "No, there isn't."
- Sector 5: in the twin, four still figures, and one moving: Mira, waving the launch-film wave with a bandaged hand.

**Does she know?** Not until the light. She is not a spy. Copies want only what their original wanted. **What does she
want?** To be told what to do, and to believe the disc "was made as a hello". She argues it honestly. At the light she
learns what she is and makes her first choice of her own. *If Mira dies during the run,* the count simply matches from then
on, Send it home closes, and Aris adds her name to the list twice.

**Why it is not a cliché.** Its model is *Solaris* (Hari, the copy who learns what she is). What is ours: no impostor hunt,
no evil reveal, the computer she trusts was the only witness and was right all along, and the loss lands on a friendship the
player has watched all game, Mira and A.U.R.A.

## The opening: the launch report

Crowds, the five on a podium, a reporter. The pad speakers, in A.U.R.A.'s voice: "Five crew aboard. All accounted for." The
reporter: "Nobody at the programme will say what the 2029 call said." Mira waves with a bandaged hand, and the reporter says
she "sleeps linked to the ship". Jaxon, to the camera: "Ten gets the new drive. We missed it by one." The report plays again
after every ending, and by then every line means something.

## At the light: the choice

A.U.R.A.: "I've set our course for the light, Commander." The twin waits beside it. Vance asks her one last time: which pod
does she not count? A.U.R.A.: "Pod five. It was open and empty when I woke you." Mira understands first: "It's me." Aris
writes "Mira Chen" on her list of the dead while Mira watches. Then the crew moment, with "Not yet" offered first. The
question is: **where does the disc go?**

## Against the ten tests

**1** One answer: Earth sent its address by mistake, and the light wants it. **2** Six links, each changing what the crew
does. **3** Fair play: bandage, link, "five aboard", D—, dry pod, stitches, the sure launch officer, the sign, all before the
twin. **4** One deduction per scene. **5** "Four" ends on Mira. **6** Three rules; A.U.R.A.'s one strange number is true.
**7** Cost seen first: hull 6, the station's launch officer. **8** The last hopper, and hull 8's mistake avoided by hand.
**9** Disc, count, new drive, torch, tether, letters, Aris's list, launch report all pay off. **10** Every ending takes a
friend, the hello or a lifetime, by hand, after a crew moment.

## For writers only

**Timeline (Earth years).** 1977: the disc launches on Voyager 1 (Voyager 2's record plays no part). 2029: the call is
heard. 2040–2064: hulls 1–9. 2065: hull 10. 2076: a programme ship fetches the record back. 2077: hull 1,977; hull 1,978
months later. About 2140: the last hull, 41,207. **Local years** (Earth's calendar where each ship lands): the 40,000s about
1450; the station crews and hull 1,977 about 1700; the station's call, sent from about 330 light-years out, reaches Earth in
2029, and its wavefront passes sector 2 around 1840, where hull 8 chases it and we hear its tail; hulls 10–1,976 about
1750–1840; hulls 1–8 about 1820–1845; we arrive in 1851. Jaxon's daughter is born in 2056. The pulsar map gives a when as
well as a where. The twin's four figures are still because the light read those four only in part. The original Mira went
into the light. Every ship had one sleeper on the link; on every ship one copy woke, which is why hull 6 said four.
