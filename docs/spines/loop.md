# Story spine: THE LOOP (2026-10-10)

**Status:** one candidate spine, not canon. Built from your idea that humanity's own future started the Exodus, cut down
until it holds. Written against STORY_CRAFT.md's ten tests. Nothing in the game was changed.

## In three sentences

1. In 2029 Earth heard a faint radio call from far down one heading, written in the star-map language of its own 1977
   gold disc, signed "Exodus", counting 41,000 ships and ending "Four crew, all accounted for"; Earth built the programme
   it described and never told the crews.
2. Every jump throws a ship back in time, newer drives much further, so the light at the end (it looks like a sun, gives
   no heat, and reads whatever reaches it) met 41,000 of our ships in reverse order, and our slow ninth ship arrives last.
3. The call is ours: when we reach the light our computer calls home, the light repeats her words until they cross the
   distance and land in 2029, and our own voices are lost on the way.

What a player repeats after the credits: *"We started it. Our call home is the message that sent everyone. And she said
four because, by the time she sent it, one of us was gone."*

## The fixed rules (never broken, on screen or off)

- **The light.** (1) It reads whatever its light falls on; the nearer, the faster: machines in days, people over months,
  the nearest person first. (2) What it has fully read, it can make again, as often as it likes. (3) It gives no heat and
  never moves. (Repaired: "whatever touches something it made" was a stretch; its light reaches the copied worlds anyway.)
- **The drive.** Every jump throws a ship back in time; the faster the drive, the further back. Short test hops barely
  show it, which is why Earth never noticed. Hulls 1 to 9 are hand-built with the old drive; from hull 10, the new one.
- **The call.** A ship's radio carries a few light-years. Only at the light does a call get home: the light reads it and
  makes it again, thousands of times, loud. A.U.R.A.'s numbers go out as pulses in the disc's star-map language, which the
  light repeats whole; spoken words are only sound, and fade under them. (This is the only reason the message is broken.
  Repaired: "voices are read slowly" failed, because recorded voices are machine data.)
- **A.U.R.A.** Calm, exact, never glitchy. On the pad she says "Five crew aboard." After the first jump the light falls on
  her, and she drifts toward the computers it has already read, every one of which ended on "Four crew, Commander. All
  accounted for." Every other number she gives is right. (Repaired: "a line Earth built into every computer" was the
  rejected paperwork answer in a new coat.)

## The answers

- **What the light is.** Never said on screen. The writers know: it is a reader that cannot move, so it waits, and it
  looks like the warm home star every crew hoped for. That is your deceiver. It did not need to find Earth: it reached
  Earth through our call, in our computer's voice. Earth's "strange willingness" is the light's pull, carried by us.
- **Why Earth sent every ship this way.** The 2029 call gave a position in the disc's own language, and a sky-date that
  put the sender 180 light-years away in the 1840s. A call in a 1977 language from before 1977 could only come from Earth's
  own future. So Earth did what its future seemed to ask: it named the programme Exodus, built 41,000 ships, flew them all
  down that one heading, and gave every computer the call's way of speaking, last line included.
- **What Earth hid.** The call itself. The public was told "a new world at the end of this heading". Every commander got a
  sealed envelope, "open at the end of the heading", holding the printout and one order: call home from there. Earth was
  trying to make its future happen.
- **The 2029 message.** Ours, sent from the light. What arrived is A.U.R.A.'s block: the light's position in fourteen
  pulsar lines, the sky-date, "EXODUS" (our number was spoken, so it was lost), "41,000" (the beacons she counted on the
  way, which Earth read as a number to build), four seconds of a human voice nobody could read, and her last line.
- **The one disc.** The real Voyager record, never out here. We carry only a drawing of its map, kept by hull 6's commander.
  It matters three ways: it is the language of the call (why Earth believed it); it is the ruler we date wrecks with; and
  at the light, dating our own sky with it shows we are in the 1840s. The disc is the only thing in the loop with a real
  beginning. Everything else (the name, the number, the line) has none.
- **Why A.U.R.A. says "four crew".** She names all five because she sees five. She says four because every ship that
  reached the light lost one person into it, and every one of those computers ended on the same line. The light has read
  them all, and from the first jump ours drifts into agreement with them. The same line ends the 2029 call, because our
  A.U.R.A. says it there too. It is our own ending, heard early. It costs a person.
- **Your idea, AIs acting strange near the light.** Kept, small and calm: once the light has read her (sector 4), she
  starts turning the dish toward home after each jump and logging a test call. Nobody asked her to.
- **The wrecks and hull numbers.** Higher number, older wreck. Hulls 1–8 lie in order, hull 1 longest dead (about 25
  years), hull 8 a few. The first wrong wreck is EXODUS-31, the number Vance watched painted, about 50 years dead. The
  deepest are the 40,000s, four centuries dead. Fates differ; only the deepest ships reached the light, centuries ago.
  They all called home too. Their calls landed in the 1600s, when nobody had a radio.
- **The twin ship (sector 5).** In sector 4 we land on one of the light's copied worlds, and through it the light reads
  A.U.R.A. Then it makes our ship again, from her: it turns a quarter of a second after us because it is built from what
  she sends, and it has four people in its windows because she says four. They are still pictures (her last camera frame of the crew),
  never moving, because the light can only make what it has fully read, and it has not finished reading us.
- **The lost time.** After each jump the ship's clock is further behind the stars: days, then weeks. The jump into sector 3
  stalls and the clock and the stars stop agreeing at all. The number comes only at the end: the 1840s.
- **Why us (earned, not chosen).** We had the worst drive, so we arrive last, behind eight crews who mapped the early
  route. We dated every wreck by hand. Every crew that reached the light called home; we are the only one that knew what
  the call would do, and the only one whose call lands after Earth can listen.

**How the big answer is worked out, with no lecture.** Three things agree, and the player adds them up: the 2029 printout
ends on A.U.R.A.'s exact line (sector 2); its 41,000 is the number the beacon counter reaches (sectors 3 to 6, and the
reporter's "ship nine of forty-one thousand"); and our own sky dates to the 1840s, with A.U.R.A.'s plain "If I call home
from here, Commander, it reaches Earth in 2029." Nobody says "we sent it". Most players get there in sector 5.

## At the light

1. The light fills the window. Jaxon, once: "I'm reading no heat off it." The last dating, by hand. Mira: "Commander, our
   own sky is older than the disc." A.U.R.A.: "If I call home from here, Commander, it reaches Earth in 2029." A.U.R.A.
   opens the commander's envelope, as ordered, "at the end of the heading": the same printout as sector 2. Nothing new.
2. The light starts reading the crew, one at a time; the cutaway lights deck by deck. Hull 40,963 already showed this.
   It reads the nearest person first. Whoever goes out on the tether is read whole, and while it reads them, A.U.R.A.
   can take the others back out of its reach. (Repaired: "it leaves the others" had no reason.)
3. The call goes out. Each living person says one thing into it, once: Jaxon a letter to his daughter, Aris the names on
   her list, Vance the warning ("Don't send anyone. Don't build them."), Mira thanks to A.U.R.A. On screen, the light
   repeats A.U.R.A.'s block, louder each time, and the voices thin under it. The player recognises the "[voice,
   unreadable]" from sector 2. That was us.
4. The choice, after a crew moment with "Not yet" first. The 2029 message is the same in every ending.

Afterwards, if Jaxon has standing, the survivors settle on Tethys-1 in sight of the light; otherwise they drift on. No
ending goes home, explains the light, or ends on a vault. Runs that settle early never reach the loop; the history that
sent the call is the one where hull 9 reached the light.

## What the writers know (never on screen)

- Hulls 1–9 launch about three years apart, about 2050 to 2074; from hull 10 a line builds the rest, 41,000 by about 2130,
  then Earth stops: that was the number in the call. The old drive throws a ship about 290 years back over the voyage.
  We reach the light about 1846; it is about 183 light-years from Earth, so the call lands in 2029. The deepest ships
  reached it about 1440. All numbers can move together; the 2029 landing is fixed.
- Why the light wants anything: unknown even to us, on purpose. Its behaviour never changes.
- The loop has no first cause. The line, the name and the number were never invented by anyone. Only the disc was.

## Against the ten tests

One answer: yes (three sentences, every mystery is a part of it). But/therefore: yes. Fair play: every piece is on screen
before the light; the envelope adds nothing new; the twin is set up by the sector 4 copies. One deduction per scene: yes,
mostly by dating. Cuts deeper: "four" ends on a person and costs one. Fixed rules: three for the light, one wrong number.
Cost seen first: hull 2,210's settlers and hull 40,963's crew. Why us: the slowest ship, the trail, our own dating.
Every gun: disc, count, ship ten, letters, Aris's list, twin all fire; torch and sleepers step back. Ending takes
something chosen: a backed crew member, A.U.R.A., or the dead you already lost.

## Risks

- A loop with no first cause annoys some players. It is the classic paradox; say nothing about it, show the disc as the one
  real beginning.
- "Now is the 1840s" means retuning the ledger years, the dating chart and Jaxon's daughter lines together.
- Rule 1's "whatever touches something it made" is the one stretch. It must be seen on hull 2,210 before we land.
- With two or more dead, her line over-counts. That fits "never measured", but test that it reads as sad, not as a bug.
