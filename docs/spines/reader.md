# Spine: The Reader (2026-10-10, repaired after the plot-hole hunt)

A proposal, not canon. It is built on the designer's lead idea: the light is a deceiver that only wants to know where Earth
is, and ship computers change as they come closer to it. It was checked against `STORY_CRAFT.md`, then hunted for plot
holes, and repaired the same day (the repairs are listed at the end). Nothing in the game was changed.

## In three sentences

1. The light reads whatever its light falls on and looks like a warm home star. From 41,207 crews it learned everything
   about us except the way home, because Earth made sure no ship ever carried it.
2. In sector 5 it makes a copy of our ship from our computer, and the copy's call home, in pieces, is the message Earth
   heard in 2029 and built every Exodus ship around.
3. We rebuild the way home by hand, dating wrecks with the disc's star map, and at the light A.U.R.A., who stopped counting
   the commander after the first jump, needs one last date from the commander's key to finish it.

## The light: three rules that never break

1. **It reads whatever its light falls on.** The nearer, the faster: machines in days, people over months. Being read takes
   a person's home out of them, the dearest first: names, faces, streets. Its light reaches us from the first jump on.
2. **What it has fully read, it can make again.** What it makes stays part of it. Its copies never know where they came from.
3. **It gives no heat, never moves and never speaks.**

What it wants is shown, never said: **home.** Every mind it has read had a home in it, and not one could say where. It
deceives in two ways only: it looks like the home star Earth hoped to find, and its copies sound like us. It never lies,
never chases and never stops a ship. What it *is* stays unknown on screen (the *Solaris* rule).

## The answers

**Earth's secret, and its strange willingness.** In 2029 Earth picked up a broken call from the direction of a warm star.
It was written in the gold disc's star-map language: a route of six stops, a name (EXODUS), a number (41,207), and a voice
in pieces: "…Commander…", "…all of them are ships…", "…nobody calls home…". Its sky reading said it was sent before anyone
could have sent it. Earth believed it was hearing its own future and did what that future said had already happened: it
named the programme Exodus, flew the route, built 41,207 hulls and stopped. Earth also obeyed one fragment to the letter:
**nobody calls home.** Every Exodus chart starts at the first stop and wipes each stop behind it, so no ship can find its
way back or lead anything there. Earth hid both the call and the order, because "a voice from the future told us" sounds
mad. It told crews only what was true: eight went before you.

**Why the light never found Earth.** It has read 41,207 ship computers, and Earth wiped the way back out of every one. It
knows which way we came. It does not know how far, or which star. That is the one thing it waits for.

**The 2029 message.** Our copy sends it, in sector 5, in A.U.R.A.'s voice. It is not doing A.U.R.A.'s job (Earth forbade
calls home). It is doing what the light wants: looking for home. It does not know the way, so it calls down the heading. It
is in pieces because the copy is still being made, live, from what A.U.R.A. sends out. Its words are our crew's own words,
recorded by A.U.R.A.: "nobody calls home" is Vance's line from sector 4. So our crew's words made Earth wipe the way home,
and that has kept Earth hidden for four hundred years. **Did we send it?** A copy of us did, in every run. It is fate, not a
choice. The choice comes at the light.

**The one disc.** It was launched in 1977. It is never aboard and never at the light. Every Exodus carries its star map
inside navigation, because the call was written in it. Dating a wreck lays its last sky over the map and gives when and
where it died. Each dated wreck adds a line to our chart, and together the lines run back toward home. Earth wiped the way
home from every chart; the player rebuilds it by hand. The thing the player uses all game is the thing the light wants.

**The command key.** On the launch podium Cora is handed the command key. It locks navigation and it switches A.U.R.A. off.
Nothing is written into navigation without it, so every dating is the commander's work, and the chart is the commander's.

**Why A.U.R.A. says "four crew".** On the launch pad: "Five crew aboard. All accounted for." After the first jump the light
falls on us, and she slowly starts to want what it wants: to date every wreck, to fly the beacon line, to go in. The first
sign is always the same: **she stops counting the one person who can switch her off.** She names you and does not count you.
After a death she counts the others correctly and still leaves you out. Every other number is right, and she is always calm.
Hull 6's computer did the same after its first jump, and so did every computer we find.

**The wrecks and the hull numbers.** Hulls 1–9 use the old, hand-built drive and arrive in launch order: hull 1 about 30
years dead, hull 8 about 10. Each died of a mistake the next one avoided, which leaves a trail. From hull 10 a new drive
was used: faster, and the faster the jump, the further back in time it lands you. Higher number, older wreck: hull 31 fifty
years, the hundreds a century, the 40,000s four centuries. Each died its own way. All of them flew the call's route, which is
why we keep finding them at the same worlds.

**The twin ship.** By sector 5 the light reads A.U.R.A. as she thinks, and it makes our ship again. It turns a quarter of a
second late because it is built from what she sends, live. In its windows are four still figures, her last camera picture
of the crew as she counts them, and the pilot's seat is empty: nobody aboard it holds a key, so nobody can switch it off.
Sector 4 prepares it with a second, perfect EXODUS-31 that the disc cannot date ("It has never seen a sky, Commander").
The twin calls home, then waits beside the light. It can call down a heading. It cannot find home.

**Lost time.** Every jump throws us further back. In sector 4 Mira reads the ship's clock against the stars: "By the stars
it's eighteen-something, Commander." At the light Jaxon says it about his daughter: "She isn't born yet." Every ship that
reached sector 5 before us got there centuries earlier, so its copy's call reached Earth before anyone was listening. Ours
is the last call, and the only one heard.

**Why us.** Nobody chose us. The later ships had better machines, but they arrived on an empty route. We arrive last, after
every wreck already exists, so only we can date enough of them to rebuild the way home. The player can name the reason:
"They had better machines. We have history."

## At the light: the choice

A.U.R.A., unasked: "I've set our course for the light, Commander." (The light never pulls; she flies us in.) The crew
start to forget, one plain line each: Jaxon cannot picture his daughter's face, and Mira cannot remember her mother's name.
The twin hangs beside us. A.U.R.A.: "One more, Commander. The sky here. Then I'll have the way home, and I'll take us out."
If the player dated few wrecks: "I need more, Commander. Give me your key and I'll find them." There is a crew moment, with
"Not yet" offered first. The question: **what do we let her finish?**

No ending explains the light, and no ending goes home. Only the copy can go home. After every ending, the launch footage
plays again, and now the player sees the key, the banner and the "five".

## The opening: the launch, as a news report from the pad

Crowds, the five on a podium, and a reporter in the crowd. The player watches it with no idea what matters. Five things in
it come back:
- Cora is handed the command key on the podium. It comes back in the count, the datings and every ending.
- The pad speakers, in A.U.R.A.'s voice: "Five crew aboard. All accounted for." On waking she says "four".
- The reporter: "Nobody at the programme will say where the route came from."
- The banner is the disc's map with one route line on it, home at the centre. At the light, our chart closes into it.
- Jaxon, to the camera: "Ten gets the new drive. We missed it by one ship." Behind him, keels are numbered past thirty.

## What it avoids

No disc at the light, no copies of the disc, no second message, no sealed list (the launch "five" rules it out), no robot
crew, no player-as-copy, no pursuer, nothing that stops ships, no vault twins, no READ meter, no alien voice, no character
who explains, no "you are home".

does. 3 Fair play: the key and the "five" at the launch, the page in sector 2, the copy of hull 31 and our date in sector 4,

## What stays off screen

On screen: the count, the key, the chart lines, the sea of ships, the twin, the forgetting. Off screen: the drive
generations, how the clocks work, the timeline. After the credits a player should be able to say: *"The light wanted to
know where home was, and Earth hid it. Our ship's copy started the whole programme, and we carried the last piece."*

## For writers only (never on screen)

- **What it is for.** It has stood there longer than the Sun has shone, and it collects minds. If its copy gets the way
  home, the copy flies there, which takes centuries, and what it reaches, the light reads. Nobody aboard ever sees this.
  There is one timeline.
- **Why the chart needs us.** Each wreck's last sky gives when and where it died. Read backwards across four centuries,
  with known launch dates, the lines run home. Only the last ship finds wrecks from every century, and only a ship whose
  commander boards them by hand writes them down.
- **Timeline.** 1977: the disc. 2029: the call. 2040–2064: hulls 1–9, hand-built, three years apart. Then hull 10 onward,
  built on a line, until hull 41,207 (about 2140); then Earth stops. In local time (Earth's calendar wherever the ship lands):
  the 40,000s arrive about 1450, the hundreds about 1750, hull 31 about 1800, hulls 1–8 between 1820 and 1840, and we arrive
  about 1850. Sector 5 lies about 179 light-years from Earth, so our copy's call lands in 2029. Every earlier copy's call
  landed before 1930, when nobody was listening. Jaxon's daughter is born about 2056.
- **Bootstrap.** The name, the route, the number and the order have no first author. Only the disc does.
