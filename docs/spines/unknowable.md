# Spine: THE UNKNOWABLE (2026-10-10, proposal)

One complete story spine built on your "keep it unknown kind of, the structure that wants knowledge". Like *Solaris*: we
never learn what the light is or why it does anything. What it does never changes, so the player can feel its logic. The
mystery stays open, but the story closes: Earth's secret, the count and the last choice are clear. Nothing in the game was
changed. Tested against `docs/STORY_CRAFT.md` and the rejections in `docs/STORY_IDEA_BANK.md`.

## In three sentences

1. A light at the end of our heading reads everything near it and makes copies of what it has read, and on every ship
   that ever reached it, it took the person nearest to it first: the commander, whose seat on every Exodus is alone in the
   bow window (it is drawn there in the cutaway from minute one).
2. Earth sent us there because of a broken message in the gold disc's star-map language that gave a route, an impossible
   date and the number 41,207, and A.U.R.A. has said "four crew" since our first jump because every ship's computer
   near the light ends that way.
3. At the light the player decides who will be nearest: you, as every commander before you, or someone else, or nobody
   (then all five slowly forget home), and A.U.R.A.'s last count says what that cost.

## The light: three rules that never break

1. **It reads whatever its light reaches.** Machines in a moment, even from far away. People slowly: the nearer, the
   faster, and the nearest person first. Being read takes home out of a person: names, faces, where they came from.
2. **What it has fully read, it can make again.** Its copies are exact, except that they never know where they came
   from: a garden with no roots, a planet with no star, a ship with no commander.
3. **It gives no heat, never moves and never speaks.** It does not pull, chase, lie or bargain.

**On screen:** nobody ever says what it is or what it wants. The player sees the three rules work, again and again.

**What the writers know (never shown):** it finishes things. It reads until it can make a thing again, and keeps what it
makes. People are never finished, because it reads them through what they remember, and nobody remembers all of home. So
it never stops; it reads the next nearest one. It does not want Earth; it has nothing we would call a want. From Earth it
looks like a warm sun because its light takes about 180 years to reach Earth: by then it had read crews hoping for one.
That is the "deceiver": it never lies, it looks like hope. Its machine reading has one side effect: computers near it slowly
become like the computers it has already read. That is A.U.R.A.'s "four".

## The ending at the light

**Approach.** A.U.R.A., unasked: "I've set our course for the light, Commander." Her one act of the light's will; she is
calm. It fills the windows; Jaxon: "I'm reading no heat off it." It reads her whole in a moment; she says nothing new. "Four crew, Commander. All accounted for."
(Repaired: the built "Five crew" here contradicted her rule; "five" is kept for the endings that break the pattern.)
Then it begins on the nearest person: Cora, at the front. Mira: "It's reading the Commander first."

**The last dating.** The player dates our own sky with the disc, by hand, one last time. Mira: "Commander, our own sky is
older than the disc." Jaxon: "Then my daughter hasn't been born yet." The disc ends the game as it began it: a clock.

**The crew moment.** Each person says one line about who should be nearest. "Not yet" is offered first. A.U.R.A.'s report
goes out before the choice in every ending; Vance records "Do not come" into it. The player who remembers the sector 2
tape knows no words arrived. Closing image of every ending: her report on screen, a row of star fixes, a date, 41,207.

## The ten tests

1. One answer: it takes the nearest, and everything we were told came from our own report. All six boxes are faces of it.
2. But / therefore: the six scene sentences above.
3. Fair play: the banner number, the five on the pad, the copies, the twin's dark window and the graves are all seen before
   the light. The last scene adds no fact; the Tethys log only confirms. The twin is prepared for by sector 4's copies.
4. One deduction per sector, mostly by dating; the count is confirmed by three objects that agree (channel, twin, graves).
5. Cuts deeper: "why four" ends on the player, and every ending costs a named person something.
6. Three rules; A.U.R.A. wrong about one number, always four, calmly. Motive unknown on screen, written down here.
7. Cost seen first: the settlers' blank names (sector 4) and the Tethys four before it can happen to us. We are never the copy.
8. Why us: last arrival, whole record, built by the player's own dating. No luck, no chosen one.
9. Every gun fires: disc (last dating), count, "ten gets the new drive" (hull 31), sleepers (the bow), Aris's list (her
   ending), Jaxon's daughter (not born), the tether (Aris and Vance). The torch stays a hatch tool. The reportage re-reads.
10. Every ending takes something chosen, through the tether, the dating or the standing, after a crew moment.

## Risks

- The player can guess "the commander goes" by sector 5. That is the Hitchcock bomb: the dread is the point, and the choice
  of who is nearest is not guessable.
- The 1850 answer is heavy. On screen it is two lines (Mira, Jaxon); everything else is writers only.
- Six endings is a lot of writing; the front seat, Aris and Turn back are the three to build first.
