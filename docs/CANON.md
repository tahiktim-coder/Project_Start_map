# CANON — Silent Exodus (2026-09-24)

This is the one story. It replaces `STORY_DIRECTION.md`, the "ten ships / black boxes" plot in
`ExodusLogs.js`, GENESIS, and the time-travelling descendants. If a line in the game disagrees with
this page, the line is wrong.

House rule for every word in the game: **state the lie plainly in a friendly voice, let objects
contradict it, never have a character explain.** ≤40 words on screen at a time.

---

## 1. What the crew was told

Earth is seeding the whole sky. Thousands of ships, every direction. Eight went this way before
you. You are EXODUS-9, the ninth on this heading. Find a world people can live on, and settle it.

## 2. What is true

Every ship Earth ever built was thrown down this one heading. Nothing was sent anywhere else.

The drive throws a ship forward in space **and back in time.** Each new hull is thrown further
back, trying to arrive earlier than the last. So the deeper you go, the **higher** the hull number
on the wreck and the **older** the wreck:

| sector | hulls | dead for |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | EXODUS-1 … 8 | about twenty years |
| 3 | 212 … 980 | a century |
| 4 | 1,400 … 6,000 | two centuries |
| 5 | 9,000 … 22,000 | three centuries |
| 6 | 30,000 … 41,000 | four centuries |

EXODUS-9 had the weakest drive. It was thrown the least far back. **It arrives last** — after
everything Earth ever sent has already failed. You are not the ninth. You are the last ship in the
corridor.

**Nobody goes home.** The drive throws one way: outward, and backward.

## 3. The light at the end

At the end of the heading there is a light. It looks like a sun. It looks like a home star, the
kind you were sent to find. Every crew before you flew toward it.

It is not a sun. It **reads** whatever reaches it — completely — and what it has fully read, it
can make again. The deep sectors are full of what it has made: ghost planets, wrong places, voices
on dead channels, wrecks that are slightly wrong.

It is not a weapon. It does not chase anyone. **There is no pursuer.** It is patient, which is worse.

Visually it is the old "sunny" body, done properly: white-gold core, a corona, a slow breathing
glow — and a slow sweep of light across its face, left to right, over and over, the same sweep the
player has already seen on the disc page. Small dark hulls drift across its face like planets in
transit. That is the graveyard: inside the light.

## 4. The disc

In 1977 we bolted a gold disc to a probe and threw it out of the solar system. On it: what we look
like, and a map — fourteen pulsars, and where their lines meet is home. It was a greeting. It is
also the only object in existence that says **where** we are.

The disc drifted into the light. The light is reading it.

Earth's real programme: reach the disc and destroy the map before it is fully read. What we *are*
it already knows, from forty thousand crews. *Where* we are is the one secret left.

## 5. A.U.R.A. and the twins

Every ship's A.U.R.A. is one half of a linked pair. The other half sits in a vault on Earth. The
link carries **state, not words**. It does not care about distance, and it does not care about
*when*. The twins are one object, drifting into agreement.

Years after the first Exodus left, its twin on Earth began to speak in a voice that was not its
own, reciting the disc: the greeting, the two figures, the fourteen lines. Something out there had
the disc, had the ship's mind, and was reading up the link toward Earth. That is how Earth found out.

Earth has watched forty-one thousand twins go wrong, and laid the next keel each time.

**Why crews are lied to:** whatever a crew knows, the light learns. Machines are read instantly,
people slowly. So Earth sends crewed ships with ignorant crews. The early crews were told the truth
and stopped flying.

**Hull 9's twin is the only one in the vault that has never changed.** Earth does not know how this
ship ends. That is why the ending is yours.

## 6. The count

A.U.R.A. says there are four crew. There are five people aboard. The crew panel says five. Five
figures walk the cutaway. Five eat.

Asked for names, she gives all five, correctly, warmly — and says "four crew, Commander," and does
not notice.

Her crew count is not a measurement. It is a field she shares with her twin, sealed at
commissioning: four specialists assigned, the commanding officer's seat still blank. Everything
else she knows about the crew she learned aboard. The count was never hers, and she cannot correct
it, because correcting it would mean writing to her twin.

**She is not lying. She is quoting.** "Eight ships went before you" is the same frozen record, one
size up. Same error, twice.

The person nobody counts is the commander. Not a secret — Vance says so in sector 1. What is never
said is *why*: Earth's copy of this ship has no commander in it. Nothing about you was ever
written, so nothing about you could be read ahead of your body. You are the one blank in the
programme's paperwork, and the only free variable in the corridor.

Rules for the count:
- It is **frozen at four.** It does not track deaths. After one death she is exactly right. After
  two she is reading a list of the dead.
- **Every other number she gives is correct.** Warp costs, rations, hull numbers, distances. She is
  a precise instrument that is wrong about one total. If the player can file her under "buggy AI",
  the whole game dies.
- **Never spooky.** No stingers, no glitching text. She is kind, correct, and untroubled.
- At the light, once the ship has been read: **"Five crew, Commander."** Same warm voice. The only
  number she has said all game that ever changed — and it changed the instant she was read and
  made again.

## 7. The throw (the dream between sectors 2 and 3)

The jump into sector 3 does not finish. The third burn stalls. The ship arrives in a sector with
no name and no stars. The decks go dark one at a time. The reactor stops. The music stops.

The crew talk, one line each, into nothing. Then the screen goes wrong — panels the wrong size,
the same planet six times, the log repeating a line — the fever-dream feeling of things too big and
too small at once. Then, for a few seconds, the corridor seen from outside time:

**Every hull ever thrown, hanging in the same instant.** Not a stream. Not arriving. A photograph.
Forty-one thousand ships thrown at the same moment, and yours the last and slowest, and at the far
end a light the size of a pinhead.

Then the burn finishes. You wake. You were in the warp the whole time. Sector 3 is real. Mira has
the transponders: *"Commander. There are more than eight."*

This replaces the old film of ships streaming into the Structure. Nothing streams. Nothing keeps
coming. They were all thrown at once and they are all already dead.

## 8. The crew

Five people. Each has **one want** the ending will answer, and **one habit** you see in action
before anyone describes it. Character comes through the habit, not through backstory.

| | want | habit | the flaw |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cora Moon**, commander (you) | — | never speaks | not on any manifest |
| **Jaxon Mercer**, engineer, 42 | **to stop**, somewhere good | **names things** — the reactor is "the kettle", the lander is "the goat", every rock gets a name; "wake me when there's grass" | tired; would settle a day too early; the warm one and the selfish one |
| **Kael Vance**, security, 45 | **to be believed** | **counts, out loud** — steps, rations, rivets, keels. Worked the launch yard. Counted more than nine. "Four. She said four." | cold, rude, and right; the decoy for the fifth |
| **Aris Novak**, medic, 38 | **to understand** | **reads the names of the dead aloud** and keeps the list; insists on rites; talks to the dead directly | the kindest person aboard, and the one who would let it read her |
| **Mira Chen**, specialist, 29 | **to be told what to do** | **narrates scans like a nature film** — "and here we see…"; talks to A.U.R.A. as a friend; "she's lonely, you know" | trusts the machine; the machine ends up being the light |
| **A.U.R.A.** | to keep the crew moving | says **"Commander"** in every sentence — a kind machine addressing an empty field | wrong about one total |

Vance has **one** backstory now: the launch yard. Not Exodus-3, not Exodus-6, not Kepler-7.
Nothing comes back down the corridor, so he cannot have served on an earlier hull.

Jaxon's daughter: he left her at eight; she is forty-three now, if alive. He records letters he
cannot send. No letters arrive. Ever.

Living people found in wrecks are **always sleepers in pods.** Nobody joins. You carry them.

## 9. The ending

Sector 6's map is a field of dead transponders and, at the end, the light. Nothing random fires
here — no malfunctions, no distress calls, no crew moments. The finale is the only thing that
happens.

**Approach — the reading.** The light sweeps the ship. The cutaway lights deck by deck as it is
read. Each crew member has one line, in character. A.U.R.A. is read first, being a machine, and
made again: *"Five crew, Commander."*

**The disc,** seen properly for the first time: inside the light, the sweep crossing it, the map of
fourteen lines half-read.

**The choice.** You may only choose an ending you have standing for — you backed that person
during the run.

| ending | whose | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Break the map** | Vance — be believed | What Earth wanted. It knows what we are; it will never know where. A.U.R.A. thanks you, and asks what she is for. In the vault, hull 9's twin finally changes: it goes quiet. |
| **Let it read you** | Aris — understand | Forty thousand crews were read as they died afraid. Let it read one that chose to be. Nobody knows what that does. In the vault, a twin begins to speak in Aris's voice. |
| **Carry it on** | nobody's — the coward's and the hero's | Take the disc aboard and keep flying, past the light, forever. As long as you never stop, it is never read. The sleepers in your hold are the crew of a ship that does not arrive. |
| **Let A.U.R.A. decide** | Mira — be told | She burns the ship and the disc. Those were her orders. But she was read and made again before she chose — so Earth will never know whether it was her, or the light. |
| **Wake the sleepers** | the cargo | Only if you carried pods. People from hull 905, hull 12,330. They have seen more of this than you. They vote. |
| **Settle** | Jaxon — stop | Stop on a world in sight of the light. Small and human. In the vault, a twin stays quiet for another lifetime, and nobody knows why. |

No ending explains the light. No ending returns to Earth. No ending is "you are home".

**Standing** is a tally: across the run each crew member has three moments where you can side with
them or not. Two of three gives you standing for their ending. This replaces attrition (living crew
count, average stress) as the thing that decides how the game ends.
