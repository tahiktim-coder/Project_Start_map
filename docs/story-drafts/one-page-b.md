# One page, draft B: the song (2026-10-10)

Lens, never named on screen: a beautiful song wrecks ships on its shore, and the one who lives is the one who doesn't give in. The disc is a box that can't be closed once it's opened. Eden is the place where you stay and forget home.

## 1. The story
1. Long after the first golden record, Earth sent a second, better disc into space: where we live, how we build, everything about us, a proud hello.
2. Then Earth got scared it had shown all its cards to strangers, and began sending Exodus ships to bring the disc back, while telling the crews they were going to settle a new world.
3. The later crews, the ones given the real order, died out here, and the light at the end of the road learned from their wanting to want the disc too.
4. Now the light calls to every ship with whatever that crew wants most, but we, Exodus 9, were never told about the disc, so it barely notices us.
5. We reach its heart and find the disc almost by accident, and Cora has to decide what to do with it, knowing either way something we love stays behind.

## 2. The big moments
1. **Sector 1, the head count.** A.U.R.A. reads out all five of us and says "four crew". The player feels something is off from the first minute.
2. **Sector 1, the first wreck.** The player cuts open a dead Exodus hatch; in the logbook is a folded drawing of a gold disc. Why does a colony ship carry this?
3. **Every jump, the corridor.** The player flies the heading through debris while the radio counts distress beacons: a few, then hundreds, then too many to count. We are not the first.
4. **Sector 2, the ring of ships.** Dead ships in a circle, all playing our own launch broadcast, our families waving. The first time the song is aimed at us, and it uses our home.
5. **Sector 3, the sea of wrecks.** The jump stalls, the decks go dark, and every ship ever sent hangs still on the light's shore. Vance: "How can a ship with a higher number than ours be older than us?"
6. **Sector 3, the back garden.** A lawn, a white fence and a bench float in space and grow toward our hull. The player cuts it away or burns it. The light copies home, and gets it wrong.
7. **Sector 3, becoming a spore.** Mira breathes the garden's pollen, and for one minute the player drifts as a spore through the wrecks, seeing them as the light does: people who wanted something.
8. **Sector 4, the hole.** A starless patch where the beacons switch off one by one. The player flies through it and comes out beside a planet's twin, next to a wreck sending our own distress call. Nowhere is safe from its copies.
9. **Sector 4, Eden.** A warm dome of Earth grass, the door open, everyone healed. Jaxon wants to stay. The player lets him, or drags him back to the ship. Kindness is the trap.
10. **Sector 5, the twin.** A ship exactly like ours flies beside us a beat late, then holds still: four of us wave from our stations, one seat empty. The light has started to notice us.
11. **Sector 6, the light.** It fills the windows and gives no heat; A.U.R.A. says "five crew" for the first time. In its heart, among everything the others brought, is the real disc. Every crew before us died for it, and we only came for the light.
12. **The choice.** Cut the disc by hand while the light sweeps over it: Earth stays hidden and the song stops, but everything the light made goes dark, Eden and anyone we left there too. The last sweep finds only the two people drawn on it.
    Or take it home as Earth secretly wanted: now we want it, so the light finally sees us, and it follows us home.

## 3. Under the water
- The later disc ships (after 15 or 20, nobody knows which) had a newer drive that threw them further back in time. That is why the wrecks have higher numbers and are older. The pages show it; nobody says it.
- What scared Earth was a warning sent from the future (the 2029 message). The player can find it; nobody needs to understand it.
- Exodus 9 was never part of the disc missions. Nobody aboard was lied to about the disc, because nobody aboard knew it existed.
- The light is neither evil nor wise. It copies what it learned from the crews, and it is sure of itself and wrong: grass with no roots, a fence in space, our faces a beat late.
- Open, not ours to answer yet: why A.U.R.A. says four.
